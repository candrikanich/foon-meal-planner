# Family Dinner App — Product Spec

Oct 25, 2026 · @Chris Andrikanich

## Mission

Take the weight of dinner off one person and share it with the whole family.

In most families, one person carries dinner: the weekly plan, the nightly "what's for dinner," the shopping list, and every pivot when schedules change. Others will help, but nobody steps in unprompted. This app makes it easy and expected for everyone to contribute ideas, votes, and feedback, so the planner edits a plan instead of inventing one.

**The app is not** a recipe app, a voting app, or a grocery list app. It closes the loop between them: plan, shop, cook, log, leftovers, plan again.

**Feature filter.** Every feature must pass one test: does it make it easier for more people to contribute, or lighten the planner's load? If yes, it's in. If it's just cool, it waits.

**Week-one success test.** A new household goes from download to a first weekly plan and shopping list in under 15 minutes, with family input already in it. Ongoing: Sunday planning takes about 10 minutes.

**North-star metric.** Family dinners at the table per week. Home cooking is the default (5 to 6 nights for our family); takeout is a tool for crunch nights, not a failure.

## Households and members

One adult buys and sets up the app; one subscription covers the whole household.

**Onboarding (v1, #17).** For the household's owner, right after creating it, and resumed if interrupted: who's at your table (add everyone, kids too); "Does anyone have food allergies?" (No is one tap; Yes shows the one consent sentence and allergen chips plus Other per person); "Which of these does your family make?" (popular first, category tabs, search, Show more; flagged dishes warn but stay selectable; tapped dishes copy into the library); your rhythm (planning day and time, week start, dinner time, notification preset Light, Balanced, or Engaged); what Foon does for you (skippable, see below); invite your family (skippable); then This Week with Build my week up front. "Load a sample family" on the first screen creates a demo household (four members, a kid's milk allergy, ten starter dishes, a planned week) for App Review and the curious; it asks first, since it becomes the person's one household. The onboarding flags are written with the household, in the same transaction, so a closed app still resumes setup. Someone who joins by invite skips the owner's setup.

**Terms and Privacy (#277; `terms-draft-v1` and `privacy-draft-v1` in force since the turn-on migration, `20261119000000_agreements_in_force.sql`).** Every adult agrees to the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy versions in force, and Foon logs each agreement (`accept_agreements`: versions, where it happened, app version). The gate shows only while both are in force and this person hasn't agreed to them: before Create household or Load a sample family (a sheet, then it carries on), and otherwise before the app opens, for an adult who joined, already had a household, or agreed to an older version. The first time it's "Before you start" with links to getfoon.com/terms and /privacy; after a change, "We updated our terms" with what changed (the server's one-line summary). Agree is the only way through; Sign out (or Cancel in the sheet) is the other. Kids never see it. While nothing is in force, or when Foon can't read what is, nothing is asked: enforcement is the app's for now, and the server's once no supported build lacks the gate.

**What Foon does for you (#236, built).** One short step before the invites, with two cards: "Share any recipe" (#59) and "Tonight's a mess? Foon has a plan." (#60). Each card is optional to try (Show me, which Voice Control also answers to), and one button moves on: Skip until a card's been tried, then Got it. It sells what happens, never how: no copy says AI, Apple Intelligence or model (see AI strategy). It uses the family's own dinners: the first one tapped in "Which of these does your family make?", in the picker's order, or any dish already in the library when none was tapped (a resumed setup), or a bundled example with none at all. Nothing in it waits on the network, and nothing is saved or sent.
- **Share any recipe.** An example social post for that dish (the page's counts, a shouted title, emoji, an ask, hashtags), and what lands in the family's ideas. On a phone that can run the model, the share sheet's own progress card shows while it tidies (the same messages, with no "Don't wait", since there's nothing to finish by hand; see Household AI queue → Recipe tidy-up), then the real on-device tidy-up over the parsed lines only (as #59 sends), with the same 45-second limit as a real share (#256); slow or failed, the dish as the family saved it shows instead, under one line, "Here's how it'll look once it's tidied.", so it never passes for this phone's work. A phone that can, still getting ready (the model's downloading), shows the plain read and says this phone tidies recipes once it's ready, not the family line. On any other phone, the plain read is what lands, and the card says a planner's phone in the family that can do more tidies it up (the household queue; planners only, as #59 holds). The example is never saved, and its shouted title is capitalized for the phone's language.
- **Tonight's a mess?** The real `TonightRescue.ideas` over the family's library, at 30 minutes, on a made-up tonight with everyone home that isn't in any week, so the plan doesn't change; allergy flags come from code as usual. On a phone that can, the friendly lines come through the rescue sheet's own prompt (no names, allergies or who's home), with a 6-second limit, and the code lines stay if they don't. On any other phone, including one still getting ready, the code ideas are the feature, and the card says they work on every phone. The rescue never uses the queue, so this card doesn't promise a family phone's help.
- **Who sees it.** Only the owner's setup: someone who joins by invite skips it, and "Load a sample family" fills itself in without it. It isn't saved anywhere, so an app closed on this step resumes setup from the start, as the other steps do.

**Profiles first, accounts second.** During onboarding the owner creates a profile (first name, avatar, adult or kid, parent or not, role) for each family member before anyone installs. Attendance, ratings, and favorites attach to profiles from day one. When a member installs, they claim their profile. Kids without phones still participate via "pass the phone."

**Sign-in.** Sign in with Apple for everyone, including kids on Family Sharing accounts. No passwords; private email relay.

**Invites.** Parents send them by iMessage (the share sheet) with a link plus a code (e.g. SMITH-7KQ4-M9XP: a household prefix and 8 random characters with no look-alikes). iOS doesn't reliably carry link context through an App Store install, so the code is the fallback after sign-in. Codes work once, expire in 7 days, are replaced by a newer invite for the same person, and lock after 5 wrong guesses in 15 minutes. The link is `foon://invite/<code>` until Foon has a domain for universal links. A kid's invite waits for a parent's consent by email (#275): until it's active, the invite section asks for it (see Kids on their own devices), and once it is, the parent invites as for anyone else; the code is still made in the app.

**Who can do what.** Each person has three separate settings. Who they are (adult or kid, parent or not) is kept apart from what they do in planning (planner or member).

| Setting | Values | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult or kid | Adult, Kid | Kids get push budgets, school-hours quiet time, parental consent, and no email. |
| Guardian | Parents (adults only) | Manage people (add, invite, set roles, remove), edit everyone's allergies, give or revoke allergy and parental consent. Everyone else sees these but can't change them, except that an adult can add and remove their own allergies (#285). Review what's kept about the household's kids, their reminder log and hidden comments included, and export the household (#279). Not in the export: a kid's sign-in details and push token, and short-lived server bookkeeping (pushes waiting to send, a store run in progress); support provides those on request. |
| Role | Planner, Member | Planner: build and edit the week, manage the meal library. Member: request meals, vote, rate, add to lists, confirm meals; cannot delete dishes or rewrite the plan. |

**Owner** is the guardian who holds the subscription: billing and household settings, plus everything a guardian and planner can do. The owner's profile is always a guardian.

**Rules decided**

- One household per person in v1. Multi-household (split families) is a later feature.
- Parents (guardians) invite; planning rights alone don't include inviting.
- A nanny, grandparent, or roommate is an adult who can be a planner but isn't a guardian. A teen can be a planner too; planning rights never include managing people.
- **Away status** for members who leave temporarily (e.g. college): out of default attendance and notifications, history kept, flip to "home this week" for breaks.
- **Remove from household** (guardians, on member detail): the person's account is unlinked immediately and they lose access; their ratings and cook history stay. Deleting the whole household is separate and does erase data.
- **Delete everything about a kid** (#278, built; guardians, kids only, in the kid's editor): for COPPA 312.6, a parent can have a kid's information deleted now. It's a hard delete, an exception to "never hard-delete user content" (CLAUDE.md rule 6 lists them all) (decided 2026-11-06, #278). See Risks, privacy, and outliers → Privacy for what goes and what stays.
- Owner can transfer ownership to another guardian.
- Non-iPhone household members exist as profiles others vote for; a web page may cover them later.

**Family tab.** Header with household name, emoji or photo, Founding Family badge, and subscription status (owner). Member rows show avatar, name, role, and status (Home, Away, Invite pending), with Add member and Invite. Member detail: adult or kid, guardian, and role (guardians edit), Home or Away with optional return date (counts as Home from that date on, by the household's day, in Family, the editor, and the wanted count; the status itself and the heads-up to plan their favorites aren't built), usual weekday schedule and repeating time away (everyone edits their own; guardians can override), eats separately (guardians set it), allergies and dietary preferences (guardians edit; everyone sees), Remove from household (guardians), Delete everything about them (guardians, for a kid), taste profile of favorites, dislikes, and serving notes (visible to everyone), new dishes tried this month (see Picky-eater wins), cook tally, and per-member notification preset. A "This month" family stats card near the top, with "Your year in dinners" and "Dinner memories" under it, for everyone (see Success metrics: shown to families), then "Who's pitching in" for planners (see Keeping kids engaged without nagging → Planner engagement view). Household rhythm and planning mix defaults, kitchen, food rules, and calendar awareness on this device (planners; see Calendar awareness), allergy alerts (guardians, in a family that has agreed to keep allergies; see Allergies and dietary preferences), notification presets, show tips again (everyone; see Tips), plain-English privacy summary with data export and delete household, and subscription (owner).

- **Member detail (#47, built):** every row on Family opens it, for everyone in the household. It shows status, age, parent, planning role, eats separately, invite, usual schedule and repeating time away, allergies, taste, dinners cooked this month, and new dishes tried this month (#65; left out at zero). For a guardian, the Invite section (for a profile nobody has claimed) and, for a kid with active consent, Withdraw consent sit on member detail itself, right under who they are, so "open Family, then <name>, then Invite" (or Withdraw consent) in the consent emails and pages is the whole path (moved out of the editor 2026-11-08, after build 75 testing). Edit opens the existing editor, for whoever may change something (a kid: their avatar and their own schedule).
  - **Agreements after a wording change (#277):** Family shows a card at the top for whatever waits on the signed-in person, and nothing while no new wording is in force. A parent gets "Foon updated what parents agree to for allergies" with **Review** (what changed, the sentence, **Agree**, which agrees for the household); any other adult gets "A parent needs to agree to Foon's new allergy wording" with **Read it**; an adult who isn't a parent and has allergies recorded gets "Keep your allergies in Foon?" (their own say, `own-allergy-v1`); a parent gets one card per kid signed out by a hold (see Kids on their own devices), the same card as on that kid's member detail. Wording newer than this build can show says "Update Foon to review the new wording." instead of offering Agree.
  - **Allergies** show only when the person has some, or to someone who can add them: a parent (Add or Change allergies) or an adult on their own profile once a parent has agreed to keep allergies (Add or Change my allergies). A family that answered No doesn't see them otherwise. Adding uses onboarding's chips and its one sentence: the sentence shows whenever the answer has anything in it, and Agree and save records the consent. Taking the last one away asks for nothing. The list is read fresh on opening and again before saving, and only what this parent added or took away changes, so another parent's addition or removal stays. If it can't be read, nothing can be saved. Other is one entry whatever its capitals: "kiwi" and "Kiwi" save once.
  - **Your own (#285):** an adult who isn't a parent, on their own signed-in profile, edits their own allergies one at a time with the same screen (Add or Change my allergies, titled "Your allergies"), since someone may outgrow one allergy and not the others. They see the same sentence, but the button is Save: only a parent agrees for the household, so they can add an allergy only once a parent has (allergy alerts on). Before that they see no Add my allergies, and if the agreement goes while the screen is open it says "Foon keeps allergies once a parent agrees. Ask a parent to add yours first." Taking one away never needs the agreement. The same merge applies, so a parent's change while it's open stays. Kids' allergies stay with their parents, so a kid never sees it, and a parent can still add an allergy back after an adult removed it.
  - **While an allergy agreement is outdated (#277):** existing allergies keep flagging and taking one away still saves, but Add waits. The screen says why: a parent needs to agree to the new wording (for anyone else), the adult's own agreement comes first (with **Review**, the "Keep your allergies in Foon?" sheet), or "Update Foon to review the new wording." A parent's Agree and save agrees to the new wording when this build has it. A save the database refuses for an outdated agreement (42501 with hint `agreement_outdated` or `own_agreement_needed`) says which, not "check your connection".
  - **Remove my allergies (#285)** stays beside it when they have some: one step to delete all of them and withdraw for themselves. It asks first ("Foon deletes them all and stops flagging dishes for you. You or a parent can add them back."), then deletes every allergy recorded for them; nobody else's changes, and no consent is needed, since it keeps nothing.
  - **Reaching other phones:** This Week re-reads allergies, the dishes, and who's in the family with reactions whenever it comes back, when the family changes, and before Build my week, so a new allergy, a new or removed member, or an edit to a dish's ingredients reaches the cards without a visit to Library. Allergies flag and never filter, so Build's pick doesn't change; its card does. A profile that couldn't refresh says what it shows may be out of date.
  - **Saves that overlap:** the member editor sends only what changed since that sheet opened, not since the last refresh, so it can't undo a schedule or status someone else just set. A refused save says what's known: 42501 is "a parent, or the household's owner, can make it"; PGRST116 is "couldn't find that profile, or you no longer have access."
  - **Taste** is read-only and about dishes: Favorites, Would rather skip (not for me), and How they like it served. Archived dishes are left out. Tired of it is left out, since it's a snooze that wears off.
  - **Cook tally** is this month's dinners and thank-yous, the same as the Family list. There's no row at zero: it's credit, so nobody's profile says they didn't cook.
- **Rhythm and planning mix (#47, built):** Family → Rhythm and planning mix, planners only, the same rule as the server's. It's onboarding's rhythm form plus the mix (new dishes, takeout nights, leftover nights, days before a repeat), and saves only the keys changed since it opened, so a stale phone, or a refresh while it's open, can't undo another planner's change. Changing dinner time moves tonight and every later night (see Household planning settings); the mix applies the next time Build my week runs.
  - **Week start:** can't change while a plan is running or waiting (the latest plan's last night is today or later, by the household's day), or when Foon can't read the plans. This Week finds a plan by the day it starts, so moving the start would hide a running plan's dinners and let another start over the same nights. The check runs when the screen opens and again on save, on the device only.
    - **Its weaknesses, known and accepted for now:** a family that always starts next week early rarely gets a window to change it. After a change, the new week can begin on days already behind you, which show as blank Earlier cards beside the old plan's. Two planners racing can still get past the check.
- **Allergy alerts (#67, built):** Family → Allergy alerts, a switch for any guardian, with the household settings above the reminders. Off asks first, then erases every allergy the household has (see Allergies and dietary preferences → Turning allergy alerts off); this phone's flags go at once, even if the reload after doesn't get through. On shows onboarding's consent sentence with Agree and save. A family that never agreed doesn't see it, and nor does anyone while Foon can't read the consent log. It checks again when the first allergy is saved, and on pull to refresh.
- **Notification presets on Rhythm:** the household preset, saying what each sends today and what's on the way. Family also says whether reminders are on for this device. Each person's own preset is on their member detail (see Notification presets, per person, #47). Ignore handling is on the device (see On-device reminders → Ignored prompts, #47).
- **What Foon keeps (#47, built):** the privacy summary, for everyone, next to export and delete in Your data: what's kept, what stays on the device, what leaves it, who sees what, and how to change your mind. "Removes it from Foon" describes what the app does; how long a hosted backup keeps deleted data is for the privacy notice and legal review. Each line has to be true of the code today, so a change to what the app collects or sends changes that screen in the same PR (analytics and crash reporting aren't in the app yet; when they land, "no tracking" needs another look). It isn't the privacy policy (#67) and claims no law. The drafts of the privacy policy, terms and retention policy, pending legal review, are in [legal/](legal/README.md).
  - **Subscription events (#66):** once the app buys with the household's id, Foon keeps what Apple tells it about the household's subscription: the notification's type, product, Apple's subscription id (original transaction id), environment and date, each Apple subscription's status and expiry, and on the household its status, product and expiry. No name, email, price or payment details: Apple keeps those. Only the server reads the events; the household's status is visible to its members. Events are pruned after 2 years, and deleting the household erases them and its subscriptions with it. Nothing is stored until a purchase carries the household's id; What Foon keeps says so in a line of its own (built with the client half).

**Minimal data.** Household name (last name or nickname), first names, avatars. No birthdays, phone numbers, photos, or location, apart from the household's time zone (decided 2026-10-28). That's coarse, needed to know when last night's dinner was, and disclosed in the privacy notice. The dinner memories timeline (#65, part 3) shows family photos read from the phone's library on that device only; none are uploaded or stored by Foon (decided 2026-11-03). Calendar awareness reads events on the device the same way: only their times, never uploaded or stored (#64). Keep the app a parent-run household app, not in the App Store Kids category. COPPA applies if users under 13 are known; get a legal review before launch.

**Allergies and dietary preferences**

Important, never a burden. Families without allergies don't see allergy UI, with one exception: when this phone couldn't read the library or the allergies, it can't know what the family's allergies are now, so a card or list says so. A dish it can't find says it couldn't check (see "A dish Foon can't look up"), and a dish it can find keeps what it last read and says "Allergies may be out of date", which can mean every dish card while offline, because a new member's allergy could be the one missing. Families with allergies tell the app in about 30 seconds.

- **One question in onboarding:** "Does anyone have food allergies?" No is one tap and the topic doesn't come back as a question (the one note a family with none can see is when a load fails; see "A dish Foon can't look up"). Yes opens one screen: a short plain consent ("Foon keeps these so it can flag dishes. Only your household and our hosting provider, which stores them for us, receive them. They're never sold, never used for ads, and never leave your devices for AI. A parent can withdraw anytime in Family → Allergy alerts, which erases them all.", `allergy-data-v2`, #280) above each person with tappable chips. The screen's one button, Agree and save, is the opt-in; there is no separate consent step. (Wording pending legal review.)
- Standard chips: the 9 major US allergens (milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, sesame) plus Other with free text. Each maps to a keyword list (casein and butter map to milk, tahini to sesame).
- Guardians add and change allergies later from member detail, using the same chips. Everyone in the household sees them. An adult who isn't a parent can add and remove their own, one at a time or all at once (see Member detail → Your own, #285).
- **Flag, don't filter:** affected dishes (starter pack included) stay available with a flag such as "Contains milk (Nora)." Flags show in the library, on day cards, in Swap and Plan it before a dish is chosen, and on the shopping list ("Cheese: get dairy-free"). Planning is never blocked by an allergy, and allergies never trigger push notifications.
- **No ingredients, no check (#155):** flags read a dish's ingredients, so a dish with none can't be flagged, and no flag mustn't read as safe. Wherever flags show (library tile and VoiceOver, meal detail, day cards, the freezer stash, Swap, and Plan it) it says "No ingredients yet, so allergies can't be checked" instead. Flags count sides, so a dish whose side is a library dish with no ingredients says "No ingredients for Cornbread yet, so allergies can't be checked", and one whose side isn't in the loaded library says "Couldn't find a side for this dish, so allergies can't be checked" (#185). A retired side isn't served, so it drops off and says nothing. The library reads in pages, so a household past the server's 1000-row cap still gets every dish, along with each dish's history, likes and everyone's verdicts (#189); a side should only go missing briefly, when another phone is changing dishes during a read. Meal detail looks sides up in the whole library, like the tile: it flags what's in a side, which it used to miss. It's said only when someone has allergies recorded (on a night's card, someone home that night), so a family with none never sees it. Every path to a night reads the same card: Build, Swap, Plan it, a freezer night, and a leftovers night that knows its dish. A quick pick names no dish and says nothing. It isn't a flag: those dishes stay out of the Allergy flags filter, which says how many meals can't be fully checked whenever there are any, flagged dishes or not. The shopping list flags item by item, so it can't say what's missing: a dish with no ingredients adds only its sides, and a side with none adds nothing.
- **Flags before choosing (#157):** Swap (Suggestions, Your meals, held back by food rules, the freezer, Foon's ideas, and Restore) and Plan it show a dish's flags and its no-ingredients note before it's chosen, by the card's own rule: the same function, for whoever's marked home that night (someone who eats separately counts, a removed member doesn't), sides included. Someone with allergies and no attendance row that night (added after the week was made) counts as home for allergies, on cards, in Swap and Plan it, on freezer offers, and in the list's notes, since nobody's said they're out; only being marked out takes them off. Serving notes and jobs still go by who's marked home. The list's notes count whoever's home for the dish or for its leftovers night, linked or the night after a big batch, the same as that night's card. Plan it lists several nights for one dish, so each night says its own. Flags never hide or block a dish.
- **A dish Foon can't look up (#157):** a card holding a dish the library can't find, including a freezer night, leftovers linked to it, and leftovers the night after it (only the library can say that dish was a big batch), says "Couldn't check this one for allergies. Pull down to try again." and pulling down reads the library again. While a load is running or hasn't started, it says nothing and waits for the answer, so there's no false warning. A load that ends with no answer, failed or cancelled, says it. If the last load failed, the family's allergies are unknown or may be out of date, so it says it to everyone, even a family with none recorded: that's the one time they'd see this. A dish it can still find keeps the flags from the last read and says "Allergies may be out of date. Pull down to try again.", on its card, in Swap, and in Plan it. So does a refresh that couldn't read the allergies (reactions failing alone doesn't count), since someone's allergy may be what's missing; pulling down does a full load, and the note stays until its answer is applied, while a dish it can't find waits for that answer. If the last load worked and the library is just missing the dish (another phone just planned it), it's said only when someone home has allergies recorded. Plan it says the same when the library couldn't be read, since it has the dish but not its sides or the allergies.
- **Leftovers that name no dish say nothing (#157):** leftovers someone picked by hand after a known dish that isn't tagged a big batch stay silent, on the card and the list, because Foon doesn't know what's in them. So do Build's fallback leftovers night on a rushed night and leftovers after a quick pick. This silence is a known limit, not a check.
- **Turning allergy alerts off** (Family → Allergy alerts, any guardian, #67) erases the household's allergy data. It asks first ("It erases every allergy recorded for your household, for everyone. This can't be undone."), then `revoke_allergy_consent` revokes the household's consent and the database deletes every allergy in it; the consent log and who-agreed records stay. The allergy notes on list lines go too (#280): every note on the household's lines, bought ones included, is cleared, and while alerts are off no line keeps a note, so a stale phone can't write one back (a note is free text, so "Couldn't find it last trip" goes with them). When one person's allergies all go (removed by a parent or themselves, their account deleted, anonymized, or the kid deleted), the parts of list notes naming them, or that might (a short "(Nora +2)", or a part cut off inside its parentheses at the length limit), go, and the week asks the planner to Update the list, which puts back what everyone else needs. Deleting or anonymizing someone who had no allergies takes only the parts that name them, so the family's grouped warnings stay; see docs/legal/retention.md. Nobody can add an allergy while they're off. Turning them back on shows the same consent sentence with Agree and save (`grant_allergy_consent`), and allergies are added again on each person's profile; saving an allergy on a profile while they're off agrees too, as it always has. The switch shows only to guardians in a family that has agreed at least once (on, or turned off), so a family without allergies never sees it; such a family agrees the first time a parent adds an allergy. While Foon can't read whether they're on, the switch doesn't show.
- Later, not v1: a substitution table and "Make it safe" button that create the family's own version; a library review prompt when an allergy is added.
- Presented as a helper, not a guarantee. UI and terms say families still check labels: under the chips in onboarding and on a person's allergies, and under Allergy alerts.
- Household-only visibility, never sent off-device, disclosed in the App Store privacy label.
- Dietary preferences (vegetarian, no pork) use the same mechanism as soft warnings.

## Meal library and ideas

The library is the foundation; adding, editing, and removing meals must be nearly frictionless.

**Starter pack.** About 50 common family dinners (tacos, spaghetti, grilled chicken, breakfast-for-dinner), each pre-tagged with ingredients, effort, and prep steps. Onboarding: tap the ones you make, and the library is seeded in about 3 minutes. Untapped dishes never enter the library.

Full starter list with categories and tags: [Starter Pack](starter-pack.md)

**Catalog vs library.** The starter pack is a shared, read-only catalog we maintain and grow through updates. The family library holds the household's own meals and is what planning draws from. Onboarding asks "Which of these does your family make?" and copies each tapped dish into the library as an editable version (rename, tweak, own photo). Favorites come later from ratings. After onboarding the catalog stays browsable (Library, Add, Browse ideas), appears below the family's dishes in search, and feeds the optional "try something new" slot, filtered by allergies and effort. The algorithm never fills a week from the catalog except through that slot.

**Meal imagery.** Photos first, icons as backup. Starter pack: about 50 AI-generated photos made once before launch with a shared style prompt (overhead, warm light, square crop) via an image service, checked for commercial-use terms, and bundled with the app; free-license stock photos fill any gaps. Apple's on-device Image Playground is for stylized illustrations, so it is only used for fallback tiles on capable devices. Family meals: the imported recipe's photo, then an optional family photo (prompted after a dish is first cooked: "Snap your version?"), labeled "Made by" ("Dad's tacos") and shown everywhere the dish appears, then an auto-composed tile of ingredient icons (spaghetti, meatballs, tomato, bread) on a warm background, built from a mapped icon set.

**Each meal carries**

- Name, ingredients (quantities optional), original serving count
- Effort tags: quick (under 30 min), needs grill, needs advance prep, morning start (slow cooker), beginner-friendly
- Prep steps with lead times: thaw (24 h), marinate (4 h or overnight), slow cooker start (8 h before dinner), chop ahead. A planner edits them in the dish's editor under Prep ahead: up to 10 steps, with no reminder or a time from 1 hour to 2 days ahead. The editor offers steps by keyword, and only a tap saves one: thaw for what reads like meat, the Thaw first tag or a recipe step that says to thaw; marinate for "marinate" or "marinade" (12 hours when it says overnight, otherwise 4); slow cooker for that tag or those words; and "Start the night before" (a day ahead) for anything else left overnight, like soaking beans, unless a step already says overnight or the night before (#59). A kind the dish already has isn't offered. This keyword read is the non-AI path on every device. On the review screen for a shared photo, PDF or caption, an AI tidy-up's prep steps are offered first, the same way: only a tap adds one (see Household AI queue). A step's reminder goes by its kind and lead time, so editing one step doesn't touch another's. Changing either makes it a new reminder, which goes out even if the old one already had; rewording a step without changing either doesn't. Saving a dish re-plans this phone's reminders.
- **We keep meat frozen** (Family → Kitchen, on by default, decided 2026-11-02): turn it off if you buy it fresh, and thaw steps don't send reminders or show on cards, don't hold a dish back from an earlier night in Build my week, and aren't suggested. Neither does the Thaw first tag hold a dish back. The steps stay on the dish, in its detail, in case it goes back on. It only reads a step's words, so one that says "thaw" counts whatever's being thawed. It doesn't add a thaw reminder to a dish with no step.
- History: last made, per-person reactions, comments, who cooked

**Adding a meal**

1. **Quick add:** type a name and save. Ingredients optional; an "add ingredients" badge reminds later. Ingredients typed naturally ("1 lb ground beef, taco shells, cheese") are split into items, with autocomplete from past items. (v1: Library → Add → Quick add, for planners; autocomplete offers the household's items for the ingredient being typed, keeping its amount; the new meal opens when added.)
2. **Copy and tweak:** duplicate a starter dish ("Tacos" to "Our tacos") and edit.
3. **From a link:** recipe websites parse via embedded schema.org recipe data.
   v1 (#21): Library → Add → From a link, for planners.
   - **Reading the page:** the import-recipe Edge Function fetches the page and reads its JSON-LD Recipe: name, servings, ingredients, steps, and image URL.
   - **Preview:** before adding, the app shows the recipe scaled from the recipe's servings to the family's size (amounts like "1 1/2 cups" scale; "to taste" doesn't), with the link kept as the source.
   - **No recipe data:** the page title and link are saved, and the preview says there was no recipe card.
   - **Page can't be read:** if it's down, or the site turns Foon away, "Save just the link" keeps the link, and the preview says Foon couldn't read the page rather than that it has no recipe.
   - **From an idea (#155):** Save to library on an idea with a link opens this same screen, read at once. The idea's own name, when it has one, goes on the dish in every case (the planner can change it); with no name, the recipe's or page's name does. A social post with no recipe card uses the recipe in its caption, the same read as the share sheet, so an idea saved from a post can become the whole dish later. A caption is a guess, so here the ingredients can be swiped away, and "Keep just the name and link" drops what was read.
   - **Safety:** the function opens only public web pages, by name (IP-literal IPv6 hosts are refused). Private, loopback, link-local and mapped addresses are refused on the resolved addresses and on every redirect, with a 2 MB cap and a 10 s limit. The fetch resolves the name again, so a DNS server that answers differently the second time (rebinding) isn't caught; the platform's egress rules are the backstop. Only signed-in people can call it; the app's anon key isn't enough. It says honestly that it's Foon and doesn't try to get past sites that block apps.
   - **Daily cap (#116):** each account gets 30 imports a day, counted before the page is fetched (a day is a UTC day, so it resets at the same moment for everyone). Over the cap the function answers 429 and the app says "You've read 30 links today. Try again tomorrow, or save just the link." and drops the "some sites don't let apps read their recipes" note, since the site isn't the reason; Save just the link still works. In the share sheet a capped read never says the post has no recipe: the share saves as an idea as usual and the screen stays quiet. The count is per account, not per household, since it guards against one scripted account rather than sharing a family's data. Counts are kept 7 days: the daily retention job deletes older ones for every account (#286).
   - **Images:** not saved, because recipe photos belong to their sites; families add their own.
4. **From a photo, screenshot, or PDF:** text recognition, then ingredient parsing, then an AI tidy-up on a capable family device (#59; see Household AI queue → Recipe tidy-up).

## Weekly planning

The planner taps "Build my week," gets a solid draft, and shapes it. The algorithm is plain code and works on every device; AI is an optional layer.

**Household planning settings.** Planning day and time (ours: Sunday morning, before shopping), week start (the shopping day), new dishes per week, takeout nights, leftover nights, minimum days before a repeat (e.g. 10), dinner time per night. Each night keeps its own dinner time from when its week was made. Changing the household's moves tonight and every later night, in weeks already started too (#170); past nights keep theirs, since their prompts and logs used it. Nothing sets one night's time yet.

- Stored in `households.settings` (#22), every key optional with a default: `week_start` (ISO weekday, default 7 = Sunday), `dinner_time` ("18:00"), `new_dishes_per_week` (1), `takeout_nights` (0), `leftover_nights` (1), `repeat_window_days` (10). Out-of-range values fall back to the default. Planners change them after onboarding in Family → Rhythm and planning mix (see Family tab).

**Attendance per night.** Each night has a checklist of who's expected home, pre-filled from per-person recurring defaults ("Owen works Mon to Thu") and edited for exceptions. Planners own attendance; real-life changes happen by text and get reconciled by the day-of check-in and the after-dinner prompt.

- v1 (#22): `members.attendance_defaults` is `{"usually_away": [ISO weekdays]}`, set on the member's profile ("Usually home for dinner"; everyone edits their own, parents anyone's). Starting a week (`create_week`) makes seven nights from the shopping day at normal effort and the household dinner time, with a row for everyone not removed: out on their usual nights away, and away members out until their return date (all week without one). Someone added later gets their nights filled in when a planner next opens the week. A week starts on the shopping day; a dinner time that isn't HH:MM falls back to 18:00. Tapping an avatar toggles it; tapping the effort chip cycles normal, rushed, full evening, event.

- **Away dates (#52):** a planner marks when the whole household is away, in Family → Trips and seasons (first and last night, both included, up to a year at a time; dates that overlap or run on from each other become one stretch). The confirmation shows the dates, with the year if it isn't this one, how many nights, and what comes off. "Today" is the household's day, wherever the device is. An older household with no saved time zone gets the device's zone from the first planner's device to load it; until then each device goes by its own day, not the server's New York fallback.
  - **The nights:** from tomorrow on, everyone's out and anything planned comes off, locked nights included, and isn't restored. A request planned for one of those nights goes back to open. Tonight keeps a dinner it already has, since it may be eaten by now, and still reads as itself; a planner leaving before it swaps it off, and then it's like the other away nights, with everyone out. Tonight with nothing planned counts as away. Past nights and dinners logged don't change.
  - **On This Week:** everyone sees a note with the dates, and those nights' cards just say Away, with nothing to tap. A week with no nights left outside the dates, not yet started, says you're away instead of offering Build my week.
  - **Planning:** Build my week, Fill, Shuffle, and Plan it skip those nights (no dated requests or traditions land on them), and they don't count as open nights. The server refuses a new dinner on an away night from any device, including one that hasn't heard about the dates or an older app's Swap; the app says the family's away and reloads the household, so it learns the dates. Past nights can still be corrected, and tonight's dinner changed if it has one. I want... won't send a request dated for an away night, and an idea doesn't count as landed on one.
  - **Lists:** the weekly list leaves them out, and with nothing planned there's nothing to approve. Marking dates never removes list lines, because a line can belong to another week, be a staple, or have someone's own note merged in; the confirmation says the list keeps what's on it until someone taps Update the list. An approved week with nights still ahead offers Update the list, even when no dinner is left and even after the dates end. The quick list stays open for anything added by hand.
  - **Ending early:** removing a stretch, or part of one, puts usual schedules back on its nights from today on. They're empty until someone plans them.
  - **Stored:** `households.settings.away_dates` (`[{"from", "to"}]`), changed only by `add_away_dates` and `remove_away_dates`. Those merge into the list on the server under a lock on the household, so a device with an old copy can't undo another planner's dates. A trigger on `households` enforces it: a direct edit or settings save keeps the dates as they are, and a new household can't start with any. Up to 40 stretches can be added; removing always works. Devices reload the household quietly when the app comes back to the foreground: offline, nothing changes and no error shows. Older apps see those nights as Nobody's home.
- **Repeating time away (#49):** for shared custody and the like, a member's schedule can repeat: every other week, every three or four weeks, the whole week or Friday to Sunday, from a first night away. Each week is the seven nights from that first night, so a handoff can fall midweek, and nothing's away before it. Friday to Sunday asks for the first weekend instead, and counts from that weekend's Friday whichever day is picked, so a weekend started on its Saturday isn't split. Whoever can edit someone's usual days can edit this: themselves, or a parent. Like the usual days, it fills in each new week and leaves weeks already started alone. Stored in `members.attendance_defaults.away_pattern` (`{"every_weeks": 2, "from": "2026-11-02", "days": [5, 6, 7]}`, no days meaning all). A saved schedule keeps it with the rest. From the app, that column merges by key: a key left out stays, null takes any key away. So an older app saving the usual days, or a phone with an old copy, can't undo a pattern. A hand-written value that isn't exactly that shape is no pattern, on the server and in the app alike: it never puts anyone away.
- **Saved schedules (#52):** a parent saves everyone's usual nights home under a name ("School year," "Baseball season," "Summer") and switches between them, in Family → Trips and seasons.
  - **Saving:** copies each member's usual schedule as it is on the server right then. Using a name again, whatever the capitals, replaces that one, after a confirmation. The name is up to 40 characters.
  - **Switching:** sets each saved member's usual schedule, for weeks started from then on; weeks already started don't change. Someone added since keeps their own. If today's schedule isn't saved anywhere, the confirmation says so. The one already in use shows a check.
  - **Who:** parents save, switch, and delete, the same rule as changing someone else's usual schedule by hand. Everyone in the household can read them on the server, but only parents see the screen.
  - **Stored:** `attendance_templates`. Deleting a saved schedule, after a confirmation, removes it. A person's copy leaves every template when they're removed from the family, which deleting an account and retention both go through. It's included in the export.

**Night effort.** Each night is set to full evening, normal, or rushed. Rushed nights only get quick meals, takeout, or leftovers.

**Calendar awareness (#64).** Optional and per device: a planner turns on Family → Spot busy evenings, which is what asks for calendar access (EventKit has no read-only access, so it's full access to events; Foon only reads). It's off by default and only shown to planners, since only they change effort. So a parent's calendar only helps on that parent's device.
- **What counts:** any event on the device's event calendars (all of them; no per-calendar choice yet) overlapping the night's dinner window, from 90 minutes before dinner to 60 minutes after, on the household's clock with the night's own dinner time. All-day events, events marked free, declined and cancelled events don't count. The window is a default in code (`CalendarBusy`), not a setting.
- **What it does:** on a night still ahead (tonight until its window ends) at normal effort, with someone home and not away, This Week's card and the night's detail suggest "Busy evening? Mark it rushed". Tapping it sets the night rushed through the usual effort change; the x dismisses it for that night on this device. It never changes effort on its own, never shows on rushed, full evening, or event nights, and Build my week doesn't read the calendar.
- **What's kept:** nothing from the calendar. Events are read fresh when This Week loads or comes back to the foreground, as start and end times with no titles, and held in memory only. They're never saved, cached, synced, logged, or counted, or put in the widget or reminder snapshots. The device keeps only the switch and the dismissed night ids, and signing out clears both. Turning it off stops reading at once; access denied or restricted shows a line pointing to Settings. It works the same on the Mac.

**Build my week algorithm**

1. **Lock** what's decided: dated requests, event nights, pinned meals, standing traditions, then someday requests several people want (on a night they're all home).
2. **Fill special slots:** leftover night after a big-batch meal; takeout on the busiest night; new dishes on full-attendance nights.
3. **Filter candidates per night:** drop dishes inside the repeat window or snoozed; dishes disliked by anyone home that night; retired dishes; non-quick meals on rushed nights; grill or advance-prep meals where they don't fit.
4. **Score and draw** with weighted randomness: days since last made (higher is better), a favorite of someone home (big boost), ingredients already on hand (boost), overall thumbs-up (small boost), in season for that night (boost), a category already in the week (lower).
5. **Planner edits:** swap, lock, shuffle any unlocked night, approve. Approval generates the weekly shopping list.

v1 rules (#24, `Foon/Core/Planning/BuildMyWeek.swift`, a pure function of the inputs and a seed):

- **Kept nights** (never changed): locked, event, nights before today, and a dated request's night. Requests skip the filters because the family asked out loud. Kept nights count toward the takeout, leftovers, and new-dish settings, so a mid-week rebuild doesn't add extras.
- **A new dish** is a meal on trial. A dish that's never been made isn't new (onboarding copies dishes the family already makes); it's just due for a turn. New dishes are capped at the setting and go to full-attendance nights first; past the cap only when nothing else fits.
- **Takeout** goes on the busiest night: rushed first, then most people home, then not the shopping day, then earliest. **Leftovers** follow a big-batch meal (tagged leftovers) in the week, else land on the most rushed night.
- **Filters:** in rotation (not archived or retired), not snoozed, not already in the week, outside the repeat window for that night, not disliked by anyone home, quick only on rushed nights, enough lead time for advance prep (thaw first counts as 24 hours; a night has 24 hours per day from today plus about 8 before dinner today), no equipment the household doesn't have (see Kitchen equipment), and nothing that looks to break a food rule on that night (see Food rules). Allergies never filter; they flag.
- **Score:** days since last made for that night (1–90; never made counts as 90), times boosts for a favorite of someone home, ingredients on hand, family likes, and the season, halved for each dish of the same category already in the week; drawn with a seeded generator, so the same inputs and seed give the same week on every device.
- **Season (#54):** from each night's own date, northern hemisphere: summer is June to August, winter is December to February, spring and fall lean nowhere. In summer, dishes tagged grill or with a cold name (salad, gazpacho, wrap, BLT) have double the odds; in winter, dishes tagged slow cooker or with a soup name (soup, stew, chowder, chili) do. Only a name that says cold, chilled, or gazpacho takes a soup out of winter; "Soup and salad" is still a winter dish, and not a cold one. It's one boost however many ways a dish fits. The boost applies after the filters and only moves the odds, so dislikes, the repeat window, locks, and requests are untouched, and allergies still flag and never filter. A library with nothing in season draws exactly as it would in spring.
- **Variety (#54):** a category is a kind of dinner read from the dish's name, roughly the starter pack's: pasta, Tex-Mex, sandwiches, soup, pizza, grilling, chicken, beef, pork, seafood, breakfast for dinner. A dish tagged grill always counts as grilling, whatever its name. A dish can be in several and counts as one repeat of its most repeated category. Each repeat in the week, kept nights included, halves a dish's odds; never to zero, so a family that mostly makes pasta still gets a full week. Only this week counts, not the weeks before. Names are read in English; a name that can't be read gets no lean either way. Shuffle and swap suggestions draw the same way, except that the dish being replaced isn't a repeat; each swap suggestion does count as a repeat for the next, so the three are real choices.
- **Variety nudge (#54):** when three or more nights in a week share a category and a night ahead can still change (not locked, not an event), planners see one quiet note above the cards, for the most repeated category ("Pasta 3 times this week"; "next week" on next week's tab). The whole week counts, past nights too. It only tells; nothing is blocked or swapped. It never names a protein (chicken, beef, pork, seafood): those lean the draw, but a name can't tell a turkey burger from a beef one, so a sentence could be false. It reads only the names saved with the week, so tags don't count here. Never calories or nutrition.
- **Kitchen equipment (#55):** planners set it in Family → Kitchen: grill, slow cooker, air fryer, Instant Pot, all on until someone turns one off. A household that hasn't said keeps every dish. Each toggle saves right away.
  - **What a dish usually uses:** the Needs grill and Slow cooker tags, or a name that says so ("Air fryer wings," "InstantPot chili," "Crockpot roast," "Slow-cooked pork," "Grilled salmon," "Mixed grill," but not "Grilled cheese," "Grilled ham and cheese," "Grilled PB&J," or "Pan-grilled chicken"). Air fryer and Instant Pot have no tag: a new `meal_tag` value would stop apps already installed from reading the library. Names are read in English, and a name can be wrong either way.
  - **Build my week and Fill:** those dishes are left out, like any filter. Foon never plans one on its own: a night only those dishes could fill says Nothing fits.
  - **Never filtered:** requests, someday requests, traditions, locked nights, and past nights being logged. What the family chose lands and stays.
  - **What the planner sees:** any night holding such a dish, however it got there, carries a "Usually uses a grill" badge (the same for the others). It says usually because plenty can be made another way: chili on the stove, hot dogs in a pan. Everyone sees it, like an allergy flag; it never blocks anything.
  - **Using one anyway:** Swap offers them after everything else that fits, with the same badge, and a note under the suggestions says Search finds the rest. Search, I want..., Plan it, and traditions don't filter. Pick for me on an empty night takes one when nothing else fits, since tapping it is a choice. Shuffle won't trade a dish they can make for one they can't, but moves off one already on the night, to a dish they can make if there is one.
  - **Stored:** `households.settings.missing_equipment` (`["grill"]`) and `start_cooking_nudge`, saved only by Family → Kitchen and left out of every other settings save, so a stale device changing dinner time can't undo a planner's list. It holds what the kitchen lacks rather than what it has, so equipment added to the list later doesn't take dishes away from anyone. A value an app doesn't recognize is ignored there and kept when it saves. Two planners changing the list at once: the last save wins.
- **Food rules (#49):** planners set them in Family → Food rules: for each of meat, pork and shellfish, no rule, Fridays in Lent, Fridays, or every night. A rule belongs to the household and says nothing about why: no religion or diet is recorded about anyone. Fish doesn't count as meat.
  - **What it can recognize:** a dish's name, its ingredients, and its sides, in English, by keyword, never by AI (shellfish uses the allergy keywords). It reads "Beef tacos," "Sunday special" with ground beef in it, and a side with bacon, and it knows "Tuna steak," "Veggie burgers," "Hamburger buns," and "Goat cheese" aren't meat. It can be wrong both ways. A name and ingredients that don't say ("Grandma's special") pass. So Foon says a dish looks like it has meat, and never says one doesn't: nothing claims a dish is meatless.
  - **Not offered:** kosher and halal. They depend on how food is made and prepared, which a name can't tell, so Foon doesn't claim them and Family → Food rules says so. Nor is Orthodox Lent, which runs on another calendar. A no pork rule is only that: it doesn't make a week halal or kosher.
  - **Lent:** worked out from Western Easter (Gregorian) each year, so nothing needs updating. Fridays in Lent run from the Friday after Ash Wednesday through Good Friday. Ash Wednesday itself isn't a Friday, so it isn't covered.
  - **Build my week, Fill, Shuffle, Pick for me, and Swap's suggestions and freezer offers:** dishes that look to break a rule on that night are left out, like any filter, with no last resort: a night nothing else could fill says Nothing fits, never a wrong dish. A rule that only makes things harder leaves the night to whatever still fits: takeout comes first, and a rushed rule night takes it as any rushed night would. Then, if the week has a night a Fridays rule applies to, rushed nights draw, then those nights, before new dishes, leftovers and the rest, so a dish they could have isn't spent on Monday. A week without one draws exactly as it would with no rule. Leftovers are the same dish: a big batch isn't paired with a night it would break the rule on, in a build or a shuffle, and a leftovers night with no dish behind it stays off nights a Fridays rule applies. A kept leftovers night doesn't reach back: with a "Leftover Friday" tradition, Thursday's beef stew stays as planned, and Friday's card warns.
  - **Requests:** still land. A dated request lands on its night, since someone asked for it out loud. A someday request takes the first night it can that the rule doesn't apply to, and if everyone who wants it is only home on rule nights, it lands there. Either way the card carries the badge, and the planner can swap it.
  - **Traditions:** sit out that night, since nobody asked for it this week: the night fills as usual, and its chip says "Burger Friday sat out for a food rule". With nothing else to plan, it says Nothing fits. A quick-pick tradition isn't read as a dish.
  - **What the planner sees:** any night ahead holding such a dish, however it got there, carries a badge ("Looks like it has meat: Fridays in Lent"), leftovers and freezer nights too. Everyone sees it, like an allergy flag; it never blocks anything.
  - **Using one anyway:** Swap lists what a rule held back, with why, under the suggestions, and tapping one plans it. It leaves out what's already in the week and tonight's own dish, and shows 12 at most, saying to search for the rest. Search shows the same badge. Plan it says so against each night before the planner picks it. A pinned night, a past night being logged, and "We had something else" are left alone.
  - **Stored:** `households.settings.food_rules` (`[{"avoid": "meat", "when": "lent_fridays"}]`), written only through `set_food_rule`, which changes one food's rule in the list as it is on the server, under a lock, so a stale device can't undo another planner's. The server keeps it from any other write, a settings save or a planner's direct one, whatever they send, as it does the away dates. A rule an app doesn't recognize is ignored there and kept. It's read on the device, so an older app's build doesn't know about it.
- **Shuffle** re-rolls one unkept meal night from the saved week, keeping its kind: a big-batch night stays a big batch, a trial slot stays trial first. Quick-pick nights are changed with Swap, not shuffle.
- **Reasons:** a request, a favorite of someone home, something new, quick for a rushed night, uses what's on hand, the family likes it, "Haven't had it since yesterday / in N days / in a week / in N weeks", or "Due for a turn".

**AI layer (capable devices).** Reads the same inputs plus patterns scoring can't express ("soup when it gets cold," "Thursdays have been leftovers lately") and explains picks in plain language.

**Tonight poll.** When a plan falls apart, anyone starts a poll among people home tonight, from tonight's night: Vote on dinner, once there are two people to ask and until dinner's logged. It offers what's in the house, ideas, and the quick picks.

- v1 (#43): The sheet lists stash items through the same rule Swap uses (`FreezerStash.offers`), then backup dinners (dishes tagged Backup dinner, unless an ingredient is marked out; low still makes dinner, and an item nobody tracks counts as there), then Swap's suggestions, then the quick picks. Allergy flags show for whoever's home, there and on the vote. Backup dinners skip what the freezer skips: a dish already this week, someone home saying "not for me", a snooze, a food rule. Freezer item, backup dinner if there's none, Leftovers and Takeout start ticked, so it takes one tap. 2 to 6 options; the server reads each one itself, so nobody can type one in.
- **Closing.** 15, 30 (the default), or 60 minutes. Whoever started it or a planner can end it early. No vote counts as abstaining. One poll open at a time per night; once one's closed, another can start.
- **Who votes.** Anyone home tonight, for themselves, or for a profile nobody has claimed, since the little ones have no phones. No row in attendance means home, as with allergy flags. Someone who eats separately doesn't vote (`members.eats_separately`). Tapping again changes the vote. Someone who says they won't be home after voting stops counting.
- **The result.** Most votes wins; a tie names every winner; no votes says so. It doesn't change the plan by itself, so nobody's dinner gets voted away while they're at the store: a planner taps Make it tonight's dinner, which is a Swap like any other. So it locks the night, adds what's missing to the quick list on an approved week, and warns first when a doubled or freezer night would move. If the dish has left rotation or the freezer item's been used since, it says so and Swap is the way. The card can be dismissed per device, and goes when dinner's logged.
- **Hearing about it.** A phone sees a poll by opening This Week, which refreshes every 30 seconds while on screen. On Engaged, whoever can vote also gets a push when it starts (see Server push). No week started means no night to attach a poll to, so a planner starts the week first.

**Tonight rescue (#60).** Dinner fell through, so three ideas from what's already in the house, in the time there is. Anyone opens it with What can we make? on tonight's card and in tonight's detail, from when the night is tonight until dinner's logged, unless the family's away or nobody's home (a night with no attendance rows counts as everyone home). A poll that closed with no votes, or whose winners can't be planned any more, offers Find something quick, which opens the same sheet. No AI is needed: it's a pure, seeded function (`TonightRescue.ideas`), so the same inputs and draw give the same ideas.

- **Time.** 20 (the default), 30, or 45 minutes; changing it redraws from the same seed. The sheet says who it's for: whoever hasn't said they're out.
- **Where the ideas come from, in this order:** leftovers, the freezer, backup dinners, then quick library dishes. The first round takes one from each; the second, more backups then more dishes. Takeout, Freezer raid (only when no freezer idea made it), Fend for yourselves, then Eat out fill any gap, so there are always three. Tonight's own dish or quick pick is never offered.
  - **Leftovers:** a dinner from the last 3 days whose log said there were leftovers, unless a Leftovers dinner logged since ate them. Each says its age ("Leftover chili from Sunday · 2 days old"), around 10 minutes. Choosing it plans a plain Leftovers quick pick that names no dish (#157).
  - **Freezer:** Swap's own offers (`FreezerStash.offers`), one per dish, oldest first, around 30 minutes. Under a 30-minute budget they move to the end rather than drop out.
  - **Backup dinners:** the poll's (`PollRules.backupDinners`), within the time, with nothing to start ahead: no prep step the household does, and no thaw for a thaw-first dish when meat is kept frozen. One with no time set comes after the timed ones. Never made first, then least recently made.
  - **Quick dishes:** drawn by Build my week's filters and weights, from dishes whose own time (or Quick tag) fits, with nothing to start ahead, nothing marked out, the kitchen's equipment, and not a trial dish. The repeat window applies. On a rushed night the chosen time stands in for the Quick-tag rule. A dish with no time set is left out, since it can't promise it fits.
- **Same rules as Swap.** One "not for me" from someone home skips a dish in every pool, as do snoozes and food rules. Allergies are flagged for whoever's home, never filtered: within each pool, unflagged ideas come first. When the library's allergies may be out of date, a dish says so.
- **More ideas** draws again with a new seed.
- **Choosing.** A planner taps an idea to make it tonight's dinner. It's a normal Swap: it locks the night, adds what's missing to the quick list on an approved week, and warns first when a doubled or freezer night would move, exactly like the poll's Make it tonight's dinner. A freezer idea someone already used says so and drops out. Everyone else sees the same ideas read-only: "A planner can make one tonight's dinner."
- **Ask the family.** Anyone, when two or more are home and no poll is open, can start the tonight poll from the sheet, with the three ideas and Takeout already ticked.
- **AI wording (#60, built).** On a device that can run the model (see AI strategy), the sheet shows the code lines straight away and asks the model, on this device and never through the queue, for a warmer line per idea. The rescue is for right now, so a queued job would land too late; a device that can't run it keeps the code lines. The model sees each idea's name and a few words built in code from its kind and reason ("a backup dinner, everything's in", "a quick dish, a family favorite"), never the code line, since that can name someone ("Nora's favorite"). So it gets no names, days, ages or times, and no allergies or who's home. It's told to talk about the food, never the cook, to leave out nutrition, and to answer in the device's language. A line that's empty, longer than 70 characters, more than one line, or has a digit in it keeps that idea's code line, and the wrong number of lines keeps them all. A spelled-out number still gets through, which is why the facts always show beneath. An answer shows only on the draw it was for, the same ideas in the same order with the same code lines, so changing the time, More ideas, or closing the sheet cancels it, and a draw already answered isn't asked again. The friendly line sits under the title with the code line below it as usual, so the facts stay. Allergy flags and "may be out of date" always come from code. No spinner: nobody waits on it, and the code path is the one that always works.

**Pivots.** Swapping a night checks the new meal's ingredients against what's on hand and adds gaps to the quick list. That's for a dish on a night still to come in an approved week, since a draft has no list yet: anything not marked in stock and not already listed, with its amount, and an allergy note if someone home that night, or home for its leftovers, can't have it. An item already listed goes on again only for an allergy note none of its lines has, or to mark it for tonight when the dish is tonight's (even one only on the weekly list, so whoever's stopping on the way home sees it). A note joins whatever the line already says rather than replacing it. A night whose dinner is logged needs nothing. This Week says what was added, or that it couldn't check (before the first sync, or when the dish or the allergies can't be read), since that isn't the same as nothing missing. An item already listed keeps its own amount, so if this dish needs a different one, This Week says to check it. Each pivot line remembers which week it was for. Update the list for that week leaves off what it listed, open or bought, unless the weekly line says more: another amount (the dish was doubled, another night needs it too) or an allergy note the pivot line lacks. So an item can be on both lists, and the weekly line's amount includes the quick line's. It still shows once (see Lists below). It says nothing about another week: next week's list is built as usual. Swapping again or undoing leaves earlier gaps on the list, to remove by hand. Shuffle and Undo don't add gaps, so a list that's behind waits for Update the list, like any other change to a night. Not built yet: bumped meals offering to move to another night so ingredients don't go to waste.

With no week yet, planners get Build my week (or start with empty nights) and members get "Add your requests." A started week that's still empty offers Build my week; a partly planned one shows how many nights are open with Fill remaining nights, which drafts only empty nights. Rebuild unlocked nights redraws everything not locked; a draft that finds nothing for a night never clears what it had, and the save refuses a night another planner has locked since. Each card has lock and shuffle (Pick for me on an empty night; quick-pick nights change through Swap). Tapping a card opens the night: meal and sides, dinner time, effort, who's home, and lock. Reason chips are saved with the night so everyone sees them. Allergy badges name only who's home that night, and the first prep step shows as a badge. The cart button shows open list items and opens Lists.

**Event nights.** Holidays and dinner parties are marked on the plan and skipped by voting and ratings. Leftovers from them are still logged.

**Change history and undo (#57).** So two planners don't confuse each other. Everyone in the family can read it: a night's detail shows its recent changes, and Family → Plan activity shows the household's, one line each ("Dad swapped Tuesday to Tacos"). Only planners can undo, on the server and in the app.
- **What's logged:** a change to what a night holds, from any writer: Swap, Plan it, Build, Fill, Shuffle, Double it and its undo, the freezer, away dates, or an older app's direct write. A trigger on `plan_nights` does it, so nothing has to remember to. A whole Build or Fill is one line ("Ann planned 5 nights") and shows night by night in each night's detail. Effort, who's home, the lock, and the reason chip aren't logged. Nor are nights being created, or the plan's list.
- **Who it names:** whoever's signed in when the write is made, including inside server functions. What the system moved on its own says Foon: "Foon cleared Tuesday for away dates," and "Foon put Thursday back to Tacos when Monday's dinner changed" for a leftovers night going back, whoever swapped Monday or corrected its dinner. Someone removed reads "Former member." An entry an older copy of the app doesn't recognize isn't shown.
- **What Undo covers:** the last change on a night, if it was a plain change of pick, from the night's detail. It puts back what the night held (dish or quick pick, and lock) by the same write Swap makes, so everything that follows a night still follows it. The reason chip doesn't come back, since the log keeps no reason: one can name a person ("Wren's request"), and it would outlast them there. A request that was planned there goes back to open as after a swap. In the other direction, undoing a swap puts a request that swap sent back on the night again, if the dish is its dish. A planner's own "not this week" for that dish reads the same on the server, so it lands too. The undo is logged too.
- **What it doesn't:** it's refused, with "Can't undo that one. Swap it instead," for:
  - a night gone by in the household's day, or a logged dinner;
  - either night of a doubled pair, a freezer night, or a change that ended a link, since putting back the pick wouldn't put back the link. Undo Double it and Swap are the ways back there;
  - a dish that's since left rotation;
  - an undo, Double it, the freezer, away dates, or anything Foon moved itself. Undoing isn't a chain: only one step back, and Swap does the rest.
  An away night is refused with the words that say the family's away, even tonight holding a dinner, where a Swap still goes through. Earlier changes on a night can't be undone. The activity view is read-only: open the night to undo.
- **On the phone:** Undo only shows when it should work, and it's disabled while one's on its way, so a second tap can't come back refused after the first worked.
- **A stale phone:** the server holds the night, then checks that this entry is still the night's last and that the night holds what it says, lock included. If not, nothing changes: "That night has changed since. Here's the latest; swap it if you still want to."
- **Older apps:** their writes are logged, and nothing they do is refused because of this. They don't show the log or offer Undo. A failure writing the log never stops a plan write; that entry is just missing.
- **Stored:** `plan_changes`, written only by the trigger and read by the family. Eight weeks are kept, and the daily retention job deletes the rest. A device reads the latest 300.

**Tonight widget (#62).** Tonight's dinner on the home screen (small: tonight; medium: tonight and tomorrow) and the lock screen (a rectangle, or a line by the clock: "Tonight: Tacos").
- **What it shows:** the night as This Week shows it: the dish, a quick pick ("Takeout"), Away for the household's away dates (by This Week's rule: a dinner planned for tonight or earlier stays itself), or "Nothing planned yet". Small and medium add the dinner time. A draft shows like an approved week, as it does on This Week. Before the app has opened on this phone, or after signing out, it says "Open Foon to see the plan." A tap opens Foon.
- **Locked phones:** a dish is something the family typed, so the widget marks it private (decided 2026-11-03, Chris). On a locked phone (the lock screen, StandBy) iOS hides it and the rest still shows; once Face ID unlocks, the dish shows. A quick pick or "Nothing planned yet" isn't hidden. This keeps "a lock screen never names a dish" while the phone is locked.
- **Where it comes from:** the app writes the nights it last saw (This Week and next, from yesterday through two weeks out) to a file in the App Group whenever the week it holds changes, and asks the widget to reload. The widget has no network, sign-in or database of its own, and turns over at midnight on the household's clock. So it shows what this phone last saw: a swap made on another phone shows once this phone opens Foon. The file holds dish names, quick picks, dates and dinner times: no people, attendance or allergies. Signing out, or leaving the household, deletes it.
- **iPhone and iPad only:** the Mac has no App Group, so no widget yet. The Mac keeps the same file in its own folder, for Siri.

**Siri and Shortcuts (#62).** Three things to say, each with "Foon" in it (Apple requires the app's name). All three need the phone unlocked first, so a locked phone never speaks a dish (the widget's rule). They run without opening the app.
- **"What's for dinner in Foon":** tonight as the widget shows it, from what this phone last saw, with no network: "Tonight: Tacos, at 6:30 PM.", a quick pick, "You're away tonight.", "Nothing's planned for tonight yet.", or "Open Foon to see the plan." before the app has opened on this phone.
- **"Add to the Foon list":** Siri asks what, then puts it on the quick list as whenever, so nobody gets a push. Spoken, "and" separates things ("milk and eggs" is two), except in something the household already has ("mac and cheese"). Anyone in the family may, kids too. Things the household already has go on offline too: Siri says "Added milk. It'll reach the family when you're back online." A new item needs a connection, as on the list screen. It's the same sync as the open app, so nothing is sent twice.
- **"Request a dinner in Foon":** Siri asks what, then adds a named idea from you, as on the Ideas tab. Anyone in the family may, kids too. Needs a connection.
- Signed out, Siri says to open Foon and sign in. Signed in on a phone that hasn't synced the family yet, adding says to open Foon first.

## After-dinner loop

The plan is the log by default; one notification per person confirms it, rates it, and captures leftovers.

**The prompt.** About an hour after that night's dinner time, everyone expected home gets: "Mac and cheese tonight?" with Favorite, Good, Not for me, Tired of it, "We had something else," and "Wasn't home." Any reaction confirms the meal and records that person's rating in one tap. If anyone picks "something else," they correct the log and everyone's prompt updates. No cook assignment needed.

**First responder extras.** Leftovers: yes or no, pre-filled with the app's prediction when someone was out. Quantity isn't tracked. Who cooked: tap a family face (optional). An optional "it was off tonight" checkbox separates a bad night from a bad recipe. The optional one-line comment is for everyone who rates, not only the first; see Comment card under Prompt design.

**No responses.** Auto-confirm overnight, marked unverified so the planner can spot gaps.

**Prompt design.** A rich notification card (long-press, no app open needed): one tap on a reaction saves, no submit button. On iPhone and iPad that's a notification content extension (#122), the after-dinner card; on the Mac, the notification's own buttons (see On-device reminders). First responder gets pre-filled Leftovers and Who cooked rows. Those stay in the app, not on the notification's card (decided 2026-11-03): the card has no network, so it can't know who answered first or what was logged. "We had something else" opens a mini swap sheet, then asks whether to move the planned dish to another night. A missed prompt shows as a slim card on This Week until the next evening. "It was off tonight" lives one tap deeper. **Comment card (#149):** right after someone rates from their card, a second card asks "Anything to say about the chili?", with one short line (140 characters, line breaks become spaces), Save and Not now. It asks once, after that rating: Not now or a save ends it, and so does the app restarting. It stays on This Week until then, and if the rating's 24 hours run out first, the server refuses the save and says so. Rating isn't held up by it. What's typed is lost on switching to next week and back. Pass the phone doesn't ask, to keep it quick, so a profile with no account of its own doesn't write comments yet. The card says the comment is about the food, not the cook, and only the family sees it, which goes for kids' comments too: nothing outside the household reads them, and there's no sharing. Someone who eats separately isn't offered it, and the server refuses it. It has no way to change a comment after saving. "It was off tonight" isn't built. Reactions can be changed for 24 hours, then lock into history.

| Reaction | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Favorite | Pinned to that person; resurfaces regularly, especially on their home nights |
| Good | Default: we ate it, it was fine |
| Tired of it | Temporary snooze; out of suggestions for a few weeks |
| Not for me | Lasting dislike from that person |

**Dislike rules**

- 0 dislikes: full rotation.
- 1 dislike: suggested only on nights that person is away.
- 2 or more dislikes: retired (archived, still findable).
- Dislikes never expire on their own: a dislike stands until that person rates the dish again (their latest reaction is their verdict). Removed members' verdicts don't count. Ratings are family-only; guests don't rate. New ratings are for the last week's dinners. A retired dish stays retired unless a planner brings it back (Back in rotation, after a confirmation that the family voted it out); its dislikes still keep it off nights those people are home, and a new Not for me can retire it again. Quick picks aren't rated (decided 2026-10-28).
- **Eats separately (#49):** a parent can mark a member, usually a baby or toddler, as eating separately. They aren't asked to rate (no card, not in Pass the phone), and the server refuses a rating for them. Ratings they already have stop counting, like a removed member's: no dislike, favorite, or like, and they don't count toward retiring a dish. A dish already retired stays retired. Turning it off brings their ratings back, but doesn't re-run the dislike rule: a dish their rating would now retire stays in rotation until the next Not for me on it. They still count as home: on attendance, for allergy flags and serving notes, so the people cooking still see what's true. It's about ratings and votes, not asking: they can still be who asks for a dish in I want... A person who eats separately can be planned around but isn't asked about the food.

**Transparency.** Reactions are visible to the whole family. Always rate the dish, never the cook; the prompt asks about the food ("How was the chicken thing?").

**Cook credit.** Every logged dinner records who made it, with no rating attached. A monthly "cooked this month" tally and a one-tap "Thanks, chef" make contributions visible and encourage more cooks. Beginner-friendly tags give kids a shortlist they can make on their own.
- v1 (#46, part 1):
  - **Thanks card:** once someone is down as the cook and that's settled (the dinner's confirmed and the first responder has saved the follow-up), This Week shows "Ann cooked: Chili" with a one-tap **Thanks, chef** for everyone else, while that night's card is open. The cook sees how many thanks they got.
  - **Rules:** thanks come from your own profile, once per dinner, never to yourself, only for a cooked dish (not a quick pick), for a dinner from the last week, and are never taken back. Once someone has said thanks, the cook and the dish on that log stay as they were.
  - **Family tab:** "Cooked this month" lists each cook's dishes and thank-yous, in family order (credit, not a leaderboard), from the month's logs in the household's time zone. Quick picks don't count.
  - **Retention:** a withdrawn kid's own thanks are erased with their other contributions.

**Claim a night (#50).** Anyone can say "I'll cook Thursday" on a night's card, a planner or not, a kid or not. A claim is a promise and a log is what happened: credit only ever comes from the log.
- **Who:** you, or (in a night's detail, passing the phone) a profile nobody has claimed. A planner can't volunteer someone with their own account. Someone who eats separately isn't offered, nor is anyone marked out that night, and the server refuses both; "Who cooked?" leaves out someone who eats separately too. To cook a night you're marked out for, ask a planner to change who's home.
- **Which nights:** tonight or later, in the household's day, with nothing logged yet and something to cook: a dish or an empty night, not a quick pick. Past nights, away nights and quick-pick nights have no button.
- **Two people:** a night has one cook. The second person is told someone got there first, and the card then shows who. Tapping again changes nothing.
- **Shown:** the card shows the cook's avatar and "Ben is cooking" (or "You're cooking"). Once a dinner's logged, the card goes by the log ("Ben cooked"), and says nothing if nobody's down. A night gone by with no log says nothing.
- **Taking it back:** whoever offered, anyone for a profile nobody has claimed, or a planner, from the night's detail, until the dinner's logged. The row stays, marked released; it's never deleted. By the claim, not the night, so a phone that's behind can't end someone's newer claim.
- **When the night changes:** nothing keeps a claim in step with its night. It's read along with the night as it is now.
  - Swap, Plan it, Build my week, Fill, Shuffle, the freezer, or clearing the night: the promise stands, whatever's on the night.
  - A quick pick, including the night that becomes leftovers from Double it: on hold. The card doesn't say anyone's cooking, the night's detail says why, and it counts again if the night goes back to a dish.
  - The person marked out: on hold the same way, and someone else can take the night, which ends the old claim.
  - Away dates covering the night: it ends, and shortening the dates doesn't bring it back. Tonight keeps a dinner it already has inside new away dates, so its claim stands too.
  - Removed, deleted, or now eats separately: it doesn't count.
- **Feeding credit:** when a new log is made for a dish (the first tap, or the overnight job), its cook starts as whoever's claim stands. Confirming a saved log doesn't look at the claim again. No credit for a quick pick, or anyone marked out, removed, or eating separately. "We had something else" keeps the cook for the first responder to fix, and a quick pick doesn't count as cooking. An overnight log nobody confirmed still gives the credit from the claim: nobody said otherwise.
- **A default is a guess until it's settled.** "Who cooked?" starts on them, and the first responder can change or clear it. Their Save sends the cook only if they changed it, so a card opened earlier can't put back someone who has since been taken off. Thanks, chef waits until the log is confirmed and that follow-up is saved, on the server too, because a thanks locks the cook and mustn't lock a guess.
- **"That wasn't me":** whoever's down as the cook sees it on their own card from the start, even on an unconfirmed overnight log, and in the night's detail after the card closes. In the detail, anyone can say it for a profile nobody has claimed, and a planner for anyone. It sends only the cook, and a blank cook stays blank. "Wasn't home" also takes you off as the cook. Once someone has thanked the cook, the cook and dish stay: "That wasn't me" is no longer offered, a correction is refused with "Someone already thanked the cook", and "Wasn't home" still saves that you were out and says you're still down as the cook.
- **Timing:** a confirm doesn't lock the night, because that could deadlock with We had something else. So a claim made in the same instant can leave the cook blank, and a claim being taken back in the same instant can still name that person. Both are defaults, and can be corrected like any other.
- **Planners hear about it:** in the app for now, a note at the top of This Week ("Ben will cook Thursday"), dismissed per device, for someone else's claim on a night still ahead that stands as it is. Not one you entered yourself. Taking a claim back has no note; the card just changes. Server push (#40 car 2) sends it too, "Ben will cook Thursday.": see Server push below.
- **Retention:** a withdrawn kid's offers they made themselves are erased, and ones a parent entered stay. Deleting anyone's account, adult or kid, or removing a kid under retention ends their open claims. An adult's past ones stay as anonymous history, and a kid's stay on their profile. Export includes them.
- **Older apps** don't read claims. They log dinners through the same `confirm_dinner`, so the default still lands. Their follow-up card only shows when no cook is set, and only the first responder gets that card. So when the first responder is on an older app on a claimed night, nobody's asked about leftovers, and the dinner can't be thanked until something saves them. The cook can still say "That wasn't me" from a current app. Their Thanks card can appear early, and the server refuses it.

**Dinner jobs (#50).** Optional helper jobs: set the table, clear the table, do the dishes. Off until a planner turns one on in Family → Dinner jobs, which also says who takes turns. Everyone's in by default, including someone added later; a planner can leave someone out. Someone who eats separately is never in it.
- **Who does what:** worked out on the device from the week, so every phone shows the same thing and nothing is stored or assigned. Through the week in order, each job goes to whoever's home that night who has done least this week, then that job least. Who starts moves on each week. People home the same nights stay within one of each other. Whoever's claim stands has the night off, and nobody gets two jobs in a night. With too few people, the job done least this week goes first, so a different job doesn't show each night, not always the same one.
- **Which nights:** tonight or later, with something on the table: a dish, leftovers, takeout, or a freezer raid. Not eating out, fending for yourselves, an empty night, or an away night; those don't use up a turn.
- **What can shift:** a card says whose turn it is as things stand. Later nights that week can change when someone offers to cook or takes it back, when who's home changes on any night that week (including "Wasn't home" on a night gone by), when a night's plan changes between having jobs and not, or when a planner changes the jobs or who takes turns. Answering "Who cooked?" doesn't change anything, nor does a promised night going by, since the turns go by claims and never logs.
- **No score:** no check-off, no reminders, no tally, and nothing about who did or didn't. Can't do it tonight? Trade. It's only ever words on the card and in the night's detail.
- **Settings:** `households.settings.dinner_jobs`, written only through `set_dinner_job` and `set_dinner_job_person`, one change at a time under a lock, so two planners can't undo each other and a whole-settings save from a stale phone or older app can't touch it.

**v1 effects (#42, part 3).** A person's current verdict on a dish is their latest reaction to it:
- **Favorite:** pinned to them; Build my week boosts it on their home nights.
- **Tired of it:** out of suggestions for three weeks after that dinner.
- **Not for me:** lasting. With one person it's suggested only on nights that person is away. When two people's current verdict is Not for me, the server retires the dish (still findable under Archived).
- **Likes:** every Favorite or Good ever adds a small boost.
- **Freshness:** This Week re-reads reactions each time it loads, so last night's answers count in the next build.

**Pass the phone.** At the table, everyone rates in about 20 seconds on one device; a natural ritual and a fallback for kids who ignore notifications.

**v1 data (#42, part 1).** One log per night (`meal_logs`) and one reaction per person per log (`reactions`), visible to the whole family; neither is ever deleted.
- One tap (`react_to_dinner`) confirms the planned dinner and saves that person's reaction in one transaction.

- Anyone in the family can then correct what was eaten ("We had something else"), leftovers, and who cooked. A confirmed dinner stays confirmed, and a log saved overnight follows later changes to the plan until someone confirms it.
- People react for themselves. On a shared phone, anyone in the family can react for a profile nobody has claimed: kids without phones, or pass the phone.

- A reaction can be changed for 24 hours, then it locks.
- An hourly job saves the last week's unanswered, planned nights of approved weeks as unverified once the household's day is over.
- The household's time zone (set when it's created) decides when that is; without one, the server uses America/New_York.
- **Hiding a comment (#49, #149):** a planner can hide a comment or show it again, from meal detail. Nothing's deleted: the rating stands, and the text is kept. The family stops seeing it, its author included; planners still see it, marked, so they can bring it back. It's enforced on the server: row-level security can't hide one column of one row, so `set_comment_hidden` moves the text out of `reactions.comment` into `hidden_comments`, which only planners (and a kid's parents, for the kid's comments) can read and nobody writes directly. `meal_comments` is what the app reads, and it brings the text back for planners, and for a kid's parents on the kid's comments. ## Shopping and pantry

The plan produces one weekly list; pivots and running-out moments produce the quick list. Store-agnostic: no store setup or store logic.

**Weekly list.** Generated from the approved plan's ingredients, minus what's on hand, merged with low or out staples. A doubled night counts twice, and a night from the freezer adds only its sides (see Cook once, eat twice). Designed so one big trip covers the week.

- v1 (#26): Approve on This Week (planners) shows the item count and builds the list from the nights still ahead: each meal's ingredients and sides, merged by household item with their amounts joined ("1 cup + 2 cups"), minus items marked in stock (low still counts), plus keep-on-hand staples that are low or out. An item someone home that night can't have gets a note ("Get dairy-free (Nora)"). An item that is the allergen itself, with nothing free of it to buy (eggs, peanuts, walnuts, tofu, sesame seeds), says "Can't have it (Nora)" instead; milk, flour, and fish keep their advice, since dairy-free milk and gluten-free flour exist and fish already says "Skip fish" (#214). Approving again after a swap updates that week's list: lines it added that are no longer needed go, the rest refresh, and lines added by hand stay, gaining any allergy note alongside their own. A refreshed line's note is rebuilt from that week's dishes, but a note that came from anywhere else stays: another week's allergy note on the same item, or one added by hand (#197). A line already on the list when that shipped keeps all of its note until it's bought. A line this week no longer needs, but another week's update also listed, goes to that week, with no amount and without this week's note, until that week is updated. One with a note added by hand stays as a hand-added line with that note, once no week needs it. Another week's note alone doesn't keep a line no week needs (#209). Anything already bought for that week isn't listed again, unless it now has an allergy note the bought line didn't, since what's in the house isn't what that person can have (a pivot line also only covers the amount it bought; see Pivots). Approving next week never touches this week's lines. When allergy notes don't fit, a compact note still names every allergen ("Check: dairy, kiwi (Nora +2)"). The cart badge updates right away.

- v1 Heading to the store (#51): any adult taps Heading to the store at the top of Lists; kids can't, and they add to the list as usual. **Keep-on-hand list.** Everyday staples (cereal, milk, bagels, apples, pickles, snacks) and backup dinners (box mac and cheese, frozen pizza, pasta and jarred sauce). Each item is in, low, or out; no counting. Anyone taps low or out, on Lists → Keep on hand or in any item's sheet (from a list or a dish's ingredients), offline too. Out means it has to be bought, so marking a staple out puts it straight on the quick list, offline too, unless it's on a list already. Marked back in from low or out before anyone bought it, a line a staple put there comes off again; the server checks, so a line someone added another way stays even when the phone didn't know about it (`removeStapleLine`). Something added to a staple's line later, a pivot for tonight or a tap by hand, makes the merged line that one's, so marking the staple in leaves it and its note. Turning keep on hand back on says in too, and takes nothing off. Low is a maybe: it shows under Staples running low, one tap away. Both go on the next weekly list; approving then takes the staple's quick line off, in a pull that starts after the approve so it sees the new list, so it's listed once (a dish's "eggs, 6" covers the staple too). Backup dinners are dishes tagged Backup dinner; one with an ingredient marked out isn't in stock. Those in stock feed the tonight poll and tonight rescue.

**Pantry state without counting.** Receipt scanned: items count as on hand and staples flip to in (built, #61: a confirmed receipt checks each item off the lists and puts a keep-on-hand item back to in, listed or not; nothing else has a stock to change). Meal confirmed: its ingredients count as used. Backup dinner cooked: marked used and lands on the list to restock (not built yet).

**Offline.** The lists and the week's plan are cached on device; check-offs sync when signal returns.

**Receipts (Phase 3; forwarded email later, #237).** Photo of the receipt, or a forwarded digital receipt email. Hard part: normalizing receipt abbreviations to canonical items, improved by user corrections.

- **Receipts (#61, photo half, built).** Lists → **Scan a receipt**, for anyone in the family, kids too, since anyone can check things off. Take a photo (the iPhone's document camera, which flattens the receipt) or choose one; the Mac picks a photo. The image is read on the device by Vision (`TextRecognizer`) and dropped: never uploaded, stored, or cached, which is how "receipt images deleted after parsing" is met. No receipt or receipt line is stored either.
  - **Matching** (`ReceiptMatcher`, pure, unit-tested against Walmart, Kroger, Target, Costco, Trader Joe's and Safeway receipts): prices, totals and anything after them, tax, card and payment lines, savings, store, phone, date and address lines, department headings, and quantity and weight lines are dropped; so are lines before the first priced line that match nothing (the store's name and slogan). A line's key is its printed words without prices, codes and flags, lowercased. A learned alias for that key wins; otherwise receipt shorthand is spelled out from a small table ("BNLS CHKN BRST" is boneless chicken breast), plurals, brands, sizes and descriptions ("organic", "large") set aside, and each word compared with the household's item names: the same, a cut-short start, one reading slip, or a shorthand with the word's letters in order. 60% covering the item's words and 40% covering the line's makes the confidence; under 0.6 the line isn't guessed.
  - **Review before anything changes.** Matched lines show their item; tap to pick another or type a new one (made like Add an item, so it needs a connection), or switch a line off to skip it. Lines it couldn't place are under **Didn't recognize**, skipped unless someone picks an item. **Confirm** checks every open line for those items off both lists (as Got it does, restock included) and puts a keep-on-hand item not on a list back to in, all through the sync queue, so it works offline.
  - **Learning.** A line given a different item than suggested teaches the household: `receipt_aliases` (household, the line's key, item, who first taught it), one per key, upserted by `teach_receipt_alias` and queued like a check-off. A later correction moves it to the new item; it's never deleted, and goes with its item or household. Anyone in the family reads and teaches; other households can't see or change it. Learned aliases are fetched when a scan starts; offline the matcher runs without them.
  - **Staples.** After Confirm, up to 3 items on this receipt that aren't keep-on-hand and were checked off a list between 30 days and a day ago (an earlier trip; no receipts are kept, so the lists are the history) get "Keep apples on hand?" with Yes (keep on hand, in stock) and Not now (asks again on a later receipt). Offline, none are offered.

It runs when Foon comes to the foreground and when the plan, the family, the rhythm, or a rating changes on this device.
- **Time to plan:** planners, on the planning day and time, about the first week starting on or after that day. Not for a week that's approved, or one that's all away. On Balanced (the person's own preset, else the household's), when there are new ideas it says "2 new ideas are waiting." ("1 new idea is waiting.") in place of its usual line, under the same title, and a tap still opens This Week. New means open ideas added in the week before it goes, by the digest's rule: not theirs (asked for or entered by them), and not from someone removed. Only the count, never a dish or a name. Engaged has the evening digest instead, and Light stays as it is. Only a planner on Balanced's phone reads the ideas (when, and whose: never a dish) or keeps them; any other phone never asks, and drops any it had. A new count replaces the reminder already scheduled.

It goes to the planners home that night who have their own account, or if there are none, the adults home. Not for an unapproved week, a past night, a night nobody's home, an away night, a quick pick, or a freezer or leftovers night, and never once it's too late. Actions: Done, Remind me in an hour (not offered if an hour from then is dinner or later), and Swap dinner, which opens Swap for a planner and the night for anyone else. A step with no lead time gets no reminder.
- **Words (decided 2026-10-31):** every word in a reminder is fixed: "Dinner tonight" (or tomorrow, or a weekday), a line for the kind of step (thaw, marinade, slow cooker, or "Time to start prep."), "How was dinner?", "Tap to rate dinner.". No dish name, no step's own words, and no person's name, on any reminder or follow-up, because a family types those and a check on them can't be complete. The dish shows in the app after a tap. A family opt-in for dish names can come later. The cached reminder plan holds no dish names and no step text, only each step's kind and lead time.
- **After-dinner prompt:** whoever was expected home, at the moment the This Week card opens, for a dish. Not for someone who eats separately, once they've rated it, or if someone logged something else. Tapping opens the card. Its buttons (#122) answer it without opening the app: Favorite, Good, Not for me and Tired of it save that rating, and Wasn't home says so, the same as on the card; "We had something else" opens the card. An answer tapped with no signal is kept on the device and sent the next time the app runs, unless it's more than a week old (then it's dropped unsent) or the server has refused it for good (they eat separately, the dinner's too long ago, or there was nothing to rate). One that can't go yet only holds back later answers for the same night. An answer on the card replaces one still waiting. Wasn't home is one tap, with no "are you sure" as on the card, and shown in red. Once a night's rated on any phone, marked out, or its dish has changed, its prompt is taken out of Notification Center the next time reminders are worked out, so an old one's buttons can't write over the answer. A rating from the notification doesn't ask for a comment, and a prompt scheduled before #122 has no buttons. The lock screen still never names the dish.
  - **The card (#122, iPhone and iPad):** a long-press opens a card (the `FoonDinner` content extension, `com.andrikanich.foon.dinner`) in place of the list of buttons: "How was dinner?", the four reactions in a row, then "We had something else" and Wasn't home (red, one tap). Like the lock screen it never names the dish. A tap on a reaction or Wasn't home saves it, says "Saved.", and closes; the prompt leaves Notification Center and its follow-up is called off. It's the same answer the buttons give (`DinnerPrompt`). "We had something else" opens the app's card, as a tap on the notification does. The Mac has no card and keeps the buttons.
    - **A card answer waits until Foon next runs.** The card has no network and no sign-in, and can't wake the app. It saves the answer as a file in the App Group (`group.com.andrikanich.foon`, one file per answer, like the share sheet's inbox), and the app takes those files in and sends them the next time it runs: when it's opened, when any reminder's button wakes it (which sends every waiting answer, not only that button's), or in a background refresh that iOS runs when it chooses, never before it's asked for: from half an hour after the next after-dinner prompt goes out, so its card has had time to be answered; an hour on while a prompt that went out in the last 6 hours is still unanswered in Notification Center, so a card answered later that evening still goes out that night (asked whenever reminders are worked out, and again after each refresh, button, or trip to the background), or 15 minutes on while answers are still waiting. Until then other phones don't see it. Like any waiting answer, one more than a week old is dropped unsent. If the same night has two answers waiting, the later one wins, and a rating or Wasn't home saved in the app wins over a card answer made before it, even one not yet taken in. If there's no App Group to save to, a tap opens the app's card instead, so the answer isn't lost.
- **Ignored prompts (#47):** three after-dinner prompts in a row with nothing back turn that person's rating weekly, on that phone.
  - **What counts as ignored:** a prompt that went out and whose card has closed (a day later) with no tap, no answer from the lock screen, no rating of that night on any phone, and no "Wasn't home". Any one of those breaks the run, so answering any prompt, or rating on the card in the app, resets the count. Only nights this phone has read count (this week and next), so a run doesn't carry over from an earlier week, and it fails toward asking each night: it's only decided right after a fresh read from the server, never from the offline cache, which can't see a rating made on another phone since.

In the budget it comes after Time to plan and prep and before the check-in and the nightly prompts, since it's one a week standing in for seven, and opens a list on This Week of the week's dishes they were home for and haven't rated, each with the four reactions. Quiet hours move it to their end; it has no follow-up. Logged as `rating_roundup`.

No dish and no name, and no follow-up. A tap opens This Week, where the recap card is (see Success metrics: shown to families → Weekly recap). The week before this one is read for it alone, since by that morning it's no longer this week; nothing else reminds about a week that's over. Logged as `weekly_recap`.

When it runs short, Time to plan comes first, then prep, then the weekly rating roundup, then the check-in and the prompt, then the weekly recap, then follow-ups. A snooze goes when it was asked for.

Out west that's early, for both, until a planner's device saves the zone.

## AI strategy

The app is mostly code; Apple's on-device Foundation Models framework is a helper layered on top. No server AI, no bring-your-own subscription in v1.

**Why on-device.** Free inference, offline, private. Constraints: roughly 3B parameters, a 4K-token context, and only on eligible devices with Apple Intelligence enabled. So AI handles language tasks; scoring and planning stay in code.

**Household AI queue.** AI is a household resource, not a per-device one. Older phones submit jobs (parse this recipe link) to a Supabase queue; capable phones claim them on app open and via background tasks. Results sync to everyone. Partial results appear instantly (title and link, marked processing). If no capable device claims a job in time, the non-AI path runs. Our house: 2 capable devices, 2 not.

- **Can this device run it (#58, built).** `Core/AI/AIService` checks `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability` (an eligible device, Apple Intelligence on, the model downloaded) and makes one guided-generation call (`@Generable` output) per request, in a fresh session. Every call has a timeout (30 seconds; 20 in a background refresh; 45 for a recipe tidy-up someone is waiting on, #256). Unavailable, timed out, cancelled, a model error, or an answer that doesn't fit the shape all mean the caller's non-AI path: a call never throws, crashes, or holds the screen. The model and the availability check are closures, so tests stub them; a real model run is a device check.
- **The queue (#58, built).** `ai_jobs`: household, kind, payload, status (queued, claimed, done, failed, expired), who submitted it, who claimed it and when, the lease's end, result, error, and when it expires. Members of the household read its jobs; other households see none. A member submits a job in their own name (`submit_ai_job`). A device claims only the kinds it has a handler for (`claim_ai_job`, oldest first, `for update skip locked`, so two devices never get the same job). A claim holds for a **5-minute lease**; one that lapses (the app was closed, or iOS ended a background refresh) can be claimed again by any capable device. Only the member holding the claim completes or fails it (`complete_ai_job`, `fail_ai_job`). A job **expires 3 days** after it's submitted, and an expired job is never claimed: the non-AI result is already showing, so expiry just leaves it. An hourly job marks expired jobs and deletes finished ones 30 days on, by which time whatever used a result has its own copy. Done, failed and expired are final. It's in the export, and deleting the household erases it.
- **Who runs it.** A capable device runs a job it submits straight away. It claims the family's waiting jobs on launch and each time Foon comes to the foreground (up to 5 at a time), and an iPhone or iPad that can run the model also claims one in a background refresh, about hourly, when iOS allows. The Mac claims only when Foon opens. A device that can't run the model only submits. Results go through Supabase, so every device sees them on its next load. If the model goes away after a claim (still downloading, say), nothing is written and the lease lapses for another device; a timeout or model failure fails the job. Offline, a job isn't queued: the non-AI result stands. A device loads only the jobs it's waiting on, by id, with their status and result and never their payloads, and makes no call when it's waiting on none (#59).
- **No allergy data in a payload.** Payloads leave the device for the queue, so each kind's payload is a Swift struct built from fields that can't hold allergies or a member, and the queue takes nothing else; a unit test checks every kind. Allergy checks run on the device that shows the result, as everywhere else.
- **Kinds.** A Swift enum with a handler per kind. `echo` hands back its payload without the model, to check the queue end to end; no feature submits it. `recipe` (#59) is the first real kind.
- **Recipe tidy-up (#59, built).** For a planner's shared photo, PDF or social caption, once it's read the plain way. What the model reads is the parsed recipe lines only (title, servings, ingredient and step lines), never the raw text with the screen around it, cut at 8,000 characters; nothing parsed means nothing to tidy. It answers in a fixed shape (`@Generable`): name, servings, ingredient lines, steps, and prep steps with hours ahead (1 to 48), trimmed to what a meal holds, and held to the same limits when read back from the queue. Its instructions say to use only what the text says. The result is scaled to the family like a plain read.
  - **Here first.** A device that can run the model tidies the recipe itself, in the background, and sends nothing. Only a device that can't queues a `recipe` job, and only when the planner adds the dish: not when it's read, discarded, or added to the quick list.
  - **Waiting for it (#256).** People would rather wait 10 or 15 seconds than fix a rough recipe by hand, if Foon says what's happening. So while this device tidies, the review screen shows a progress card in place of the rough draft, and the tidied recipe replaces the card when it lands. The card's message moves on with time: "Reading the recipe…", at 5 seconds "Sorting out the ingredients…", at 12 "This one's got a lot going on. Worth the wait…", and "Almost there…" only in the last 10 seconds before the limit, so it's always true (`TidyProgress`, a pure function with tests). The copy never praises the food or says how it's done. Its animation is gentle and stops with Reduce Motion; it scales with Dynamic Type; VoiceOver hears each new message, the result and a give-up as low-priority announcements that never cut in. "Don't wait, I'll finish it myself" shows the rough draft at once, with the line that Foon is still tidying; the tidy-up carries on and fills in only what they haven't changed, before or after the dish is saved, as below. The limit is 45 seconds, not the usual 30: on a Mac, a 4,000-character recipe took 16 to 21 seconds and one near the 8,000-character cut about 42. Timed out or found nothing usable: the rough draft shows under "This one's being stubborn. Here's what Foon found. Check the amounts." If the device stops being able to run it mid-way (Apple Intelligence turned off), the draft shows without that line, since it says nothing about the recipe. A device that can't run the model shows the draft at once, as before.
  - **Planners only.** A recipe job's result goes into a dish, so only a planner's device claims, completes or fails one; the server holds the rule, and skips recipe jobs in a member's claim. So a household whose planners all have devices that can't run the model gets no tidy-up: the plain read stays, and a member's capable phone doesn't help with recipes.
  - **Never over a person's edit.** When the plain read appears, Foon notes a digest of each field (name, ingredients, steps). A result replaces a field only if it still matches, read the way a saved meal reads back, so anything changed by hand stays. A name the person gave (shared with it, or typed) is never replaced. An ingredient from the plain read that names one of the nine allergens stays if the model's lines don't name it, so a dropped line can't drop an allergy flag.
  - **Where it lands.** On the review screen if it comes back while that's open. A dish added first shows "Foon is still tidying up this recipe" and gets the result when it arrives, from this device's own run or the family device's job; only the device that captured it applies it. It reads the dish fresh and saves only if nobody has saved it since that read (`save_meal_if_unchanged`); if someone has, it reads and merges again, so their edit wins. A refusal (not a planner, the dish is gone) ends it; being offline tries again on the next open; either way it stops 3 days and an hour after the dish was added. An idea keeps the name it was saved with.
  - **Prep steps are offered, never applied.** The tidy-up's prep steps show on the review screen with the keyword ones, and only a tap adds one, so no reminder comes from a guess. A result that lands after the dish is saved changes its name, ingredients and steps only.
  - **Fallback.** Failed, timed out, or expired: the mark clears and the plain read stays, with the keyword prep suggestions (on the review screen, under the honest line above).
  - **What's kept.** A queued job's payload is emptied once it's done, failed or expired, so the recipe lines are held only until a family device has run it (3 days at most). The tidied recipe stays with the job until finished jobs are pruned 30 days on. What Foon keeps and the App Store label say so.

**Onboarding sells outcomes, not "AI."** "Share any recipe and it lands in your ideas" is true on every device; capable devices just do it better (see Onboarding → What Foon does for you, #236).

**Positioning.** AI runs on your family's own devices; allergies and information about people are never sent anywhere for AI. (Not "never leaves your phone": a recipe a device can't tidy goes through Foon's servers to another device in the family, see AI helpers in legal/privacy.md.)

**Later.** Bring-your-own AI via an MCP server exposing meals, ratings, and lists to a user's own agent.

## Technical architecture

iOS-only, multiplatform SwiftUI (iPhone, iPad, Mac) on Supabase, with a local cache for offline use.

**Client.** SwiftUI app; SwiftData cache for the plan and lists; Share Sheet extension; widgets ("Tonight: tacos"); App Intents for Siri and Shortcuts; actionable push notifications; Vision for text recognition; Foundation Models for on-device AI.

**Backend.** Supabase Postgres with every table scoped by `household_id` and row-level security from day one; Realtime for live list check-offs; Edge Functions for push notifications (APNs) that someone else triggers, recipe-site parsing, and invite codes; Storage for meal photos (receipt images are read on the phone and never uploaded).

**Why not CloudKit.** Relational queries (ratings by person, dish, and night against attendance), realtime collaboration, and a possible future web page. Trade-off: owning hosting cost and the privacy story.

  - **What each notification does:** SUBSCRIBED starts a trial (the free trial) or active; DID\_RENEW is active to the new expiry; DID\_FAIL\_TO\_RENEW is lapsed, to the end of the grace period when there is one; GRACE\_PERIOD\_EXPIRED is lapsed; EXPIRED is cancelled (lapsed when Apple gave up retrying a payment); REFUND and REVOKE are cancelled from the revocation date when they're for the current period (the transaction's expiry is at or after the subscription's), and for an earlier period are recorded and change nothing; REFUND\_REVERSED is active again; a continuing status (trial, active, or an auto-renew or extension notice) never moves the expiry earlier; DID\_CHANGE\_RENEWAL\_PREF with UPGRADE is active on the new product at once (a downgrade waits for the renewal); RENEWAL\_EXTENDED moves only the expiry; DID\_CHANGE\_RENEWAL\_STATUS only records auto-renew; TEST is answered and changes nothing. Others are ignored, as is a transaction without an original transaction id.
  - **Retries and order:** Apple retries until it gets a 200, so each notification UUID is applied once. One signed before the last applied to the same Apple subscription is recorded as stale and changes nothing. A bad signature is a 400; a failure on our side is a 500, so Apple tries again.
  - **Who writes it:** only the server. Clients read the household's status, but its subscription columns aren't in their update grant, the subscriptions and events tables have no client access, and only the service role can apply a notification.
- **Client half (#66, built).** `StoreKitClient` (Core/Services) wraps StoreKit 2; `SubscriptionStore` (Family) holds the state; `SubscriptionSection` is the UI.
  - **Buying:** only the owner (`households.owner`). Everyone else sees "Ask <owner's first name> to unlock Foon", with no buy buttons. A purchase sets `appAccountToken` to the household's id. A `Transaction.updates` listener starts at launch and finishes verified Foon transactions; Restore purchases runs `AppStore.sync()`; Manage subscription opens Apple's sheet (on the Mac, the App Store's subscriptions page). The offers say what a trial turns into ("1-month free trial, then $5.99 a month"); the footer says payment is charged to the Apple Account and renews automatically unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the period ends; Terms of Use and Privacy Policy link to getfoon.com/terms and getfoon.com/privacy (App Review 3.1.2, #282).
  - **What's offered:** a founding household (`founding = true`) sees only Foon Founding Family, instead of the standard plans; every other household sees Foon Family monthly and annual, each with its free trial shown from StoreKit's introductory offer (only to an Apple Account still eligible for it). The founding plan is never offered to a household that isn't founding.
  - **What the app knows:** `household_entitled` (RPC) plus the household's `founding`, `subscription_status`, `subscription_product_id`, `subscription_expires_at` and `subscription_auto_renew` columns, which clients may read. It refreshes on launch, on coming to the foreground, when a transaction arrives, and after a purchase or restore. The server learns of a purchase from Apple's notification, so after one the app asks it up to five times, a second apart, and until it has the plan it trusts the verified StoreKit transaction (matching this household's token) for display and for the gate. A failed read keeps what was known; unknown never blocks anything. A verified purchase whose `appAccountToken` is another household's id (this Apple Account already subscribes elsewhere) never counts here: the app says so and sets nothing. An unverified purchase says it may have gone through and points to Restore purchases.
  - **Free beta:** entitled with no subscription shows "Free during the beta", not a sell. The owner can still subscribe early from the same section.
  - **Lapsed means read-only** (only when `billing_enforced` is on and the household isn't entitled, and no verified local transaction covers it). Everyone can still see everything, export the household's data, delete the household and their account, sign out, and check things off on a shopping trip; nothing is deleted and no history is lost. Blocked, for everyone including planners: Build my week, approving and editing the plan (This Week), adding or editing library dishes (Library, and a shared dish or recipe), and adding or removing list items, staples and receipt scans (Lists), "Add all to list" and the per-ingredient plus on a dish, adding a shared text to the quick list, and Siri's Add to the List (which reads the last-known answer the app keeps in the App Group; unknown is allowed, and signing out clears it). These list paths are blocked for every member, planner or not. A banner on every tab says Foon is view-only, with Unlock for the owner (opens Family → Subscription) and "Ask <owner>" for others. One check decides it (`SubscriptionStore.canEdit(asPlanner:)`, from `SubscriptionAccess.allowsEditing`), used by This Week, Library, the share sheet and Lists. This is **client-side only for v1**: row-level security does not check entitlement, so a modified client could still write. Still open: server enforcement in the write policies; gating polls, claims, Ideas requests and Family settings, which stay open on purpose (they are the family taking part, not the planner's work).
- **`app_config.billing_enforced`** (server only, off by default): while it's off, every household is entitled. That's the free beta for everyone, founding or not. At launch a migration turns it on.
- **Entitlement** (`household_entitled(household)`): true while billing isn't enforced; otherwise true while any of the household's Apple subscriptions grants access: trial or active until 3 days past its expiry (a buffer we chose for a late renewal notice, so a lost EXPIRED can't leave a household unlocked for good; Apple's own retries run about 6.5 days, after 1, 12, 24, 48 and 72 hours), lapsed until its expiry (a grace period). Cancelled and none don't. A signed-in caller only gets an answer for their own household. What a household that isn't entitled can do (read-only, never deleting history) is the client half.
- **Ownership transfer keeps the entitlement on the household.** The old owner's Apple subscription still carries the household's id, so it keeps the household unlocked until it lapses (they cancel it in their Apple account settings). The new owner can subscribe too. Each Apple subscription is kept on its own row (`household_subscriptions`), and the household is entitled while any of them grants access, so the old one lapsing can't lock out the new one. The household's own columns show the one doing the most for it: one that grants access, the longest-running first.

## Risks, privacy, and outliers

As of 2026-10-25: Texas age-assurance rules and the amended COPPA rule are both in force, so age and consent handling belong in Phase 0, not launch week. Get a legal review before launch.

**Simplify for v1**

- Build all 12 notifications, but only 4 are on by default: plan set, prep reminder, day-of check-in, after-dinner prompt. The rest ship off and can be turned on in settings.
- Settings: presets plus advanced controls; any advanced control that can wait is flagged during build rather than cut.
- Pantry: manual in/low/out staples; receipt-driven inventory in Phase 3.
- Owner is a billing flag on a guardian, not a separate permission set.

**Security**

- Texas App Store Accountability Act: for new Texas Apple Accounts since June 4, 2026, developers handle age assurance, parental consent for minors under 18, significant-change determinations, and consent revocation. Use the Declared Age Range API, PermissionKit Significant Change API, StoreKit age rating, and server notifications ([source](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/03/apple-says-texas-app-store-age-assurance-rules-start-tomorrow-after-court-ruling/)).
- Invite codes: single-use, expiring, rate-limited, unguessable.
- RLS tests for role escalation and editing others' allergies.
- Strip location and other metadata from every uploaded photo.
- Private storage buckets with short-lived signed URLs (15 minutes). Each household has its own folder: members read it, planners add photos. Photos are re-encoded on the device, which drops location and camera details, before upload. Replacing a dish's photo deletes the old file once the dish points at the new one (`set_meal_photo` returns the old path; #286).
- Recipe-fetch Edge Function blocks internal addresses (SSRF) and sanitizes HTML.
- No allergy details or names of absent kids in lock-screen notification text.
- Sign out of all devices (Family tab) ends every session at once: the database checks each request's session, so other devices lose access immediately rather than when their token expires. Removing a member unlinks their account and ends their access immediately.

The `parental-consent` Edge Function sends two emails from `CONSENT_FROM` (info@getfoon.com), in English only:
- **Email 1, the direct notice** (312.4(c)(1)), when a parent taps the request in the app. It goes to the email Apple gave for that parent's Sign in with Apple identity, which Apple vouches for (not the account's editable email; a confirmed flag proves nothing while sign-ups autoconfirm); without one it's treated as missing. Every parent whose account has a verified email gets a **renewal notice**, "Can <name> keep using Foon?": email 1's content plus what changed (the registry's one sentence for each version that needs agreeing again since the one a parent agreed to, oldest first: v2's then v3's for a kid agreed to v1), when a parent agreed before, that the kid is signed out until a parent agrees (for a kid who never signed in on their own device, that they can't use Foon on their own device; never "signed out"), that nothing is deleted, and how to withdraw instead. If no parent has agreed after 7 days, one **reminder** goes: the same email, subject "Reminder: …". A pg_cron job pokes the function every 15 minutes while a hold owes an email. Its link opens getfoon.com/consent worded as a renewal: heading "Can <name> keep using Foon?", a What changed block, the date a parent agreed, and the buttons I agree and "No, keep <name> signed out" (No keeps the kid held and deletes nothing; the page says to withdraw consent in the app instead). For a kid who never signed in on their own device, the page and the No page never say "signed out": "<name> can't use Foon on their own device" and "No, don't let <name> use Foon". The page's what changed is the same as the email's. One parent agreeing is enough; I agree confirms by email 2 as before and links the kid's account back. The function emails its wording only while it's registered and at or after the one in force (so it can be deployed ahead of a new wording); an outdated one sends nothing (503). A parent's **Email me the form** in the app sends the same renewal notice, with the same what changed (up to the wording the function sends) and the date a parent agreed; if the kid isn't held yet (the hour between a wording change and the hold job), it says they "will be signed out soon". A hold's 7-day reminder clock starts when the first email actually goes out. After a renewal, the confirming email says the kid can use Foon again rather than "invite them now". /terms and /privacy show their version labels (`terms-draft-v1`, `privacy-draft-v1`), the registry rows adults agree to.

**Outliers to design for**

- Shared custody: recurring away patterns (every other week). v1: see Repeating time away (#49).
- Split dinners: two seatings in one night. Data model only (#49), no UI, and nothing plans with it: `plan_nights.second_seating_time` (null is one seating) and `night_attendance.seating` (1 or 2, everyone 1). It covers when and who, one dish a night; a different dish for the second seating would need a column of its own, and a list to match. Nothing writes either yet: no grants, so `create_week`, attendance and older apps are as they were.
- Babies and toddlers: "eats separately" flag, excluded from ratings and votes. v1: see Eats separately (#49). The tonight poll (#43) reads the flag.
- Recurring household food rules (no meat on Lent Fridays). v1: see Food rules (#49). Kosher and halal aren't offered: a name-based filter can't honestly promise them.
- Any household shape: single parent, couple, roommates, multigenerational, 8+ members.
- Fewer than 7 library meals: fill what's possible and suggest catalog dishes. (v1, #30: Build my week fills what it can without repeating a dish and leaves the rest open; This Week then shows, for the nights nothing fit, "Your library has N meals" (or "Nothing fit N nights" when the rules, not the count, left them open) with three popular starter dishes to add and Browse ideas.)
- Households of one and of eight or more plan like any other (#30). A night nobody's home gets no dinner (a "Nobody's home" chip, nothing on the list; Swap can still plan one). A big family's day card shows six avatars, whoever's away first, and "+N" (VoiceOver and the night's detail list everyone).
- Lapsed subscription: read-only, never delete history.
- Owner transfer and clean removal of an adult when families change.
- Planners can hide a comment. v1: see Hiding a comment (#49).
- All copy as localizable strings from day one; Spanish later.

## Brand: Foon

Working name: **Foon**, a real word for a spork (fork plus spoon). The tool is the hybrid: just as a foon does the job of a fork and a spoon, Foon does the job of a meal planner, a family vote, and a shopping list in one. Families are just families; never call them hybrid families. Short, playful, curiosity-driven; the tagline does the explaining.

## Business model and launch

One subscription per household: $5.99 a month or $49.99 a year after a 30-day free trial. No feature tiers; future add-ons decided later.
